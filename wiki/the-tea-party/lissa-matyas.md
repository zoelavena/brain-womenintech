# Lissa Matyas

> 🪑 **Seat:** researcher · **Known for:** Vice-President, Capacity Building and Innovation at ICTC; AI-exposure research showing women's jobs carry the automation risk · **Shows up at:** [[ai-and-the-future-of-women-in-tech]] (3.1) · [[pipeline-and-education]] (2.1)

## ☕ Her tea

Girl, she walked into Parliament with the receipts and named the split nobody wants to say out loud: the jobs AI is most likely to automate are the jobs women already hold — and the jobs where AI would actually help are the ones women are barely in. One honest flag: this profile rests on a single testimony and the ICTC research behind it — that's the record so far, and it's why she's brewing, not steeped.

## 👩🏽‍💻 Who she is
- Vice-President of Capacity Building and Innovation at the Information and Communications Technology Council (ICTC), a neutral, not-for-profit centre of expertise on Canada's digital economy. *(ICTC transcript, Nov 2, 2026)*
- Appeared before the House of Commons Standing Committee on the Status of Women on November 2, 2026, as part of its study on the impact of AI on women, girls, and 2SLGBTQI+ individuals, presenting ICTC's research on AI exposure across 505 occupations. *(ICTC transcript, Nov 2, 2026)*
- At ICTC she works across the talent and business development pipeline — introducing girls to digital skills, running a programme focused on Advancing Women's Leadership, and developing initiatives to equip female CEOs and business leaders to champion AI adoption. *(ICTC transcript, Nov 2, 2026)*

## 🍵 Her stories
- **The exposure split, in Parliament:** her testimony puts the numbers on the table — women hold 62.7% of employment in the 10% of occupations with the highest AI task exposure, and 64.3% of employed women aged 15–24 work in occupations where AI is far more likely to automate their core tasks entirely than to augment them. But among 55 digital occupations, women hold just 10.6% of the jobs where high AI exposure meets high potential for AI to complement human work. *(ICTC transcript, Nov 2, 2026)*
- **The flat line:** women are 32.9% of Canada's digital-economy workforce and 30% of its leadership — a number that, in her words, "has not increased over the last 20 years." *(ICTC transcript, Nov 2, 2026)*
- **Five asks, not one:** she closed with five recommendations — make AI skills development and workforce transitions central to Canada's economic strategy; accelerate practical AI adoption among women-led and small businesses; ensure AI governance supports trust and access to the full breadth of talent; strengthen women's participation in AI innovation and commercialisation through capital, research partnerships, and procurement; and expand pathways into AI research, technical leadership, entrepreneurship, and executive decision-making. *(ICTC transcript, Nov 2, 2026)*

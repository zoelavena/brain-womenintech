## 👩🏽‍💻 Who she is
- Author, public speaker, and coach for managers and leaders across the tech industry. *(larahogan.me)*
- Wrote "What does sponsorship look like?" on May 14, 2017. *(larahogan.me)*

## 🍵 Her stories
- She argues that women and nonbinary people in tech are often **over-mentored and under-sponsored**. They get plenty of advice, and advice can quietly imply they aren't ready yet. What they need is opportunity and visibility. *(larahogan.me, 2017)*
- **Her sponsorship menu:** recommend someone to lead a new project, ask them to run the postmortem, suggest they write the eng blog post or give the talk, forward their project summary to a new audience and explain why it mattered, and credit them publicly when you learn from them. *(same post)*

## 🔮 How I think she thinks
> The brain's perceived profile, read from her public work. 🟢 = she said it (cited). 🟡 = my read, inferred from the cited work.

- **She believes** anyone can sponsor, not just managers. 🟢 *(2017 post)*
- **She frames problems** as habits, not heroics. Sponsorship is a weekly routine: spot the opportunities, pick someone, learn their goals, then say their name. 🟢 *(2017 post)*
- **Her instinct when something's unfair** is to stop handing out advice and start handing over opportunities. 🟡 *(inferred from her mentorship vs sponsorship argument)*
- **What she'd tell a woman early in her career:** probably less "here's how to fix yourself", more "find people who'll put your name in the room, and be one for someone else." 🟡 *(inferred)*
- **She's careful about** advice that works for privileged people but carries real risk for others. 🟢 *(2017 post)*

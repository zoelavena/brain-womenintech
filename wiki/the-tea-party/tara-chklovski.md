# Tara Chklovski

> 🪑 **Seat:** advocate · **Known for:** Founder and CEO of Technovation; teaching girls to build technology, and courage as a trainable skill · **Shows up at:** [[ai-and-the-future-of-women-in-tech]] (3.1) · [[pipeline-and-education]] (2.1)

## ☕ Her tea

Girl, she has spent twenty years watching girls build their way into technology — and her warning is the simplest version of the AI story the brain holds: the tools are moving fast, the norms aren't, and the people left behind will be the women. Her answer is not to slow the tools down. It's to put girls on the building side of them, early, and to treat courage as something you train, not something you're born with. One honest flag: this profile is built on one long-form interview plus the nonprofit's twenty-year record — that's the voice and the substance, and it's why she's brewing, not steeped.

## 👩🏽‍💻 Who she is
- Grew up in Meerut, India; studied physics at St. Stephen's College, Delhi, and earned a master's degree in aerospace engineering from Boston University. *(EIN Presswire, Nov 22, 2026)*
- Left her doctoral programme to start a nonprofit 20 years ago, after concluding that education is the one lever that can change how much a person's birthplace determines their potential. *(EIN Presswire, Nov 22, 2026)*
- Founder and CEO of Technovation, a global nonprofit teaching girls technology and entrepreneurship, started in 2006 under the name Iridescent. Its programmes invite girls and families around the world to design technology solutions to problems in their communities. *(EIN Presswire, Nov 22, 2026)*
- Technovation's community now includes 300,000 women, and about 33,000 girls took part this season. *(EIN Presswire, Nov 22, 2026)*
- Technovation began teaching AI in 2016 and now has a decade of impact data behind its curriculum. *(EIN Presswire, Nov 22, 2026)*
- Her estimate is that roughly 70% of participants go on to technology or other STEM careers, and that a large share start businesses. *(EIN Presswire, Nov 22, 2026)*
- The goal she names is reskilling 25 million young people — modelled on the critical mass needed in each country for the Technovation experience to spread on its own. *(EIN Presswire, Nov 22, 2026)*

## 🍵 Her stories
- **The accelerator, for girls:** Technovation's programme is built on the lean startup methodology — girls identify a real problem, interview users, and launch a product within three months. *(EIN Presswire, Nov 22, 2026)*
- **What the girls build:** finalist projects from the programme's recent world summit in Bengaluru include an AI system from a team in Kazakhstan designed to reduce false alarms in mines, and a WhatsApp-based app made by 9-year-olds to collect grandparents' stories in Tamil. *(EIN Presswire, Nov 22, 2026)*
- **Opening the door without losing the room:** the programme is opening to boys while training mentors and educators so girls are not left behind. *(EIN Presswire, Nov 22, 2026)*
- **The volunteer engine:** corporate employees volunteer as mentors for about two hours a week. *(EIN Presswire, Nov 22, 2026)*

## 🔮 How I think she thinks
> The brain's perceived profile, read from her public work. 🟢 = she said it (cited). 🟡 = my read, inferred from the cited work. It's not her words.

- **She believes** societal norms are moving more slowly than the technology — and that is what will leave women behind in the age of AI. 🟢 *(EIN Presswire, Nov 22, 2026)*
- **She believes** courage can be trained — through teamwork, mentors, and building something for someone else. 🟢 *(EIN Presswire, Nov 22, 2026)*
- **She frames problems as** building problems, not critique problems: women should help build better AI rather than only critique it. 🟢 *(EIN Presswire, Nov 22, 2026)*
- **She frames problems as** a birthplace problem before a talent problem: education is the lever that changes how much where you're born determines what you can do. 🟢 *(EIN Presswire, Nov 22, 2026)*
- **Her instinct when something's unfair** is to put the excluded person on the building side of it — give a girl a real problem, users to interview, and three months to launch, and the confidence follows the work. 🟡 *(my read, from the accelerator model and the courage claim, EIN Presswire, Nov 22, 2026)*
- **What she'd tell a woman early in her career:** build something for someone else — courage is a skill you train by doing it, not a trait you wait to feel. 🟡 *(my guess, from the courage claim and the programme design, EIN Presswire, Nov 22, 2026)*

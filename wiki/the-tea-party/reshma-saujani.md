# Reshma Saujani

> 🪑 **Seat:** advocate · **Known for:** Girls Who Code, and *Teach girls bravery, not perfection* (TED, 2016) · **Shows up at:** [[pipeline-and-education]] (2.1), [[evidence-gap]] (3.2, confidence)

## ☕ Her tea

She sits at the start of the pipeline. But her big idea isn't about pipelines. It's about **who gets raised to take risks**. And that made me rethink our confidence gap (3.2).

## 👩🏽‍💻 Who she is
- Founder of Girls Who Code. *(TED)*
- Gave *Teach girls bravery, not perfection* at TED2016, and later wrote the book *Brave, Not Perfect*. *(TED; press coverage)*

## 🍵 Her stories
- **The blank screen:** in a Girls Who Code class, a student tells her teacher she doesn't know what code to write. The editor looks empty. But when the teacher presses undo a few times, the code is all there: she wrote it, then deleted it. She'd rather show nothing than show something imperfect. *(TED2016, as widely reported)*

## 🔮 How I think she thinks
> The brain's perceived profile, read from her public work. 🟢 = she said it (cited). 🟡 = my read, inferred from the cited work.

- **She believes** girls are socialized toward perfection and boys toward bravery, and that this shows up later as a gap in risk-taking. 🟢 *(TED2016)*
- **She frames problems** as culture, not capability. The student *could* code. The gap was fear of imperfection. 🟢 *(TED2016)*
- **Her instinct when something's unfair** is to build something: an org, a movement, a classroom. 🟡 *(inferred from founding Girls Who Code)*
- **What she'd tell a woman early in her career:** ship the imperfect thing. 🟡 *(inferred from "bravery, not perfection")*

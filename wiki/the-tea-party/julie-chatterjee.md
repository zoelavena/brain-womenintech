# Julie Chatterjee

> 🪑 **Seat:** advocate · **Known for:** Group CEO of Northmill Bank; breaking down technical gatekeeping so women build with AI · **Shows up at:** [[ai-and-the-future-of-women-in-tech]] (3.1) · [[exposure-gap]] (3.4) · [[sponsorship-gap]] (4.5)

## ☕ Her tea

Girl, she runs a bank and she runs the AI program inside it — and her theory of change is about who gets to touch the technology. An engineer by training who became CEO of two banks, she says the pipeline into executive roles gets built when women across every function get the confidence to build with new tools, not when they're told to wait their turn. One honest flag: her women-in-tech voice so far is concentrated in two FinTech Futures award interviews — that's the record this profile is built on, and it's why she's brewing, not steeped.

## 👩🏽‍💻 Who she is
- Master's in engineering from KTH Royal Institute of Technology, Stockholm. *(HSTalks author bio; Finextra)*
- CEO of OKQ8 Bank before moving into fintech banking leadership. *(Finextra, Sep 2024; HSTalks author bio)*
- Chief Commercial Officer and Deputy CEO of Ferratum Bank plc (renamed Multitude Bank) from 2020, and head of SweepBank, with a strong emphasis on AI and machine learning innovation, until she moved to Northmill in August 2024. *(HSTalks author bio)*
- Group CEO of Northmill Bank (Stockholm) since August 2024, succeeding Tord Topsholm (CEO since October 2021). Northmill, founded in 2006, holds a full Swedish banking licence (2019) and serves 500,000+ private customers, with branches in Sweden, Finland and Poland. *(Finextra, Sep 16, 2024)*
- At Northmill she has driven AI across software development, internal operations and customer services — including more than 2,000 welcome journeys tailored to different customer profiles — and a heavy focus on AI literacy, giving more employees the skills to use the technology in their work. *(FinTech Futures, Nov 5, 2026)*
- Peer-reviewed paper: "Maximising business value with artificial intelligence: From strategy to execution", *Journal of Digital Banking*, 10(2), pp. 106–122 (2025), DOI 10.69554/LWPA9859 — a practical framework for aligning generative AI strategy with business objectives, with ethical safeguards (compliance, transparency, bias mitigation) embedded early and enterprise-wide AI training as a core enabler. *(HSTalks abstract and citation)*
- Under her, Northmill's earnings before tax rose 56% in full-year 2025, an all-time high, and the bank has been profitable for 19 consecutive years. That is a company result, not solely attributable to the AI program — the bank presents AI as the scalability mechanism behind it. Profits were up 95% year-on-year in Q1 2025. *(Northmill FT1000 2026 release; FinTech Futures, Oct 16, 2025)*
- Won "Tech Leadership – Visionary CEO" at the Banking Tech Awards 2025; Woman in Technology nominee at the Banking Tech Awards in 2025 and again in 2026 (ceremony Dec 3, 2026, London). *(InformaConnect 2025 winners; FinTech Futures, Nov 5, 2026)*

## 🍵 Her stories
- **The gatekeeping thesis:** in her 2026 awards interview she argues the fix for women's representation runs through the technology itself — break down who gets to build with new tools, and the pipeline into executive roles follows. She puts the work at several stages at once: encourage more women into technical education, support the women already in the industry into senior leadership, and use boards' succession planning and CEO appointments to change who gets promoted. *(FinTech Futures, Nov 5, 2026)*
- **The AI program as a confidence program:** at Northmill the AI rollout isn't framed as a tools rollout — it's 2,000+ tailored welcome journeys plus an AI-literacy push so employees across functions gain the skills to use the technology in their own work. Her published framework makes the same point at industry scale: enterprise-wide AI training and prompt-engineering skills, internal champions and success stories. *(FinTech Futures, Nov 5, 2026; Journal of Digital Banking, 2025)*
- **The business case, in the boardroom's own language:** in 2025 she told FinTech Futures that diverse leadership teams understand their customer base better — and that even a board that doesn't think in cultural terms should think in business terms, since half its customers are women. *(FinTech Futures, Oct 16, 2025)*

- **What she'd tell a woman early in her career:** get close to the technology — the confidence to build is what opens the pipeline, and visibility on global stages is worth taking when it shows the next generation they belong at the forefront. 🟡 *(my guess, from the gatekeeping thesis and her visibility quote, FinTech Futures, Nov 5, 2026)*

# Brenda Darden Wilkerson

> 🪑 **Seat:** advocate · **Known for:** President & CEO of AnitaB.org; founder of Computer Science for All · **Shows up at:** [[pipeline-and-education]] (2.1) · [[exposure-gap]] (3.4) · [[sponsorship-gap]] (4.5, 5.2)

## ☕ Her tea

Girl, she builds the rooms and the on-ramps. She went from a pre-med student who stumbled into two programming classes to running the largest gathering of women in tech — and her theory of change is access: put computer science in every kid's curriculum, put women in proximity to each other, and change what companies count. She is also the person who had to cancel that gathering in 2026, which makes her the sharpest possible voice on whether the rooms survive.

## 👩🏽‍💻 Who she is
- President and CEO of AnitaB.org since 2017, a global nonprofit that connects, inspires, and strives for greater equality for women and non-binary technologists in business, academia, and government. *(All American Speakers bio; AnitaB.org descriptions)*
- A former computer scientist and software engineer — 16 years as a computer scientist before moving into education. Early in her career she was often the only woman on her team, and more often the only woman of colour. *(BusinessChief profile)*
- Northwestern University: pre-med biomedical engineering major; two required programming classes were her first exposure to computer science. *(her account, interviews with Fotis Georgiadis and Candice Georgiadis)*
- Director of Computer Science and IT Education for Chicago Public Schools, where she founded the original Computer Science for All program, building computer science into the curriculum for every student; it served as the inspiration for the Obama administration's national CS for All initiatives. She earned a White House Champion of Change award. *(IEEE / All American Speakers / Ana Melikian bios)*
- Under her leadership, AnitaB.org's membership grew from 1,600 to more than 75,000 technologists worldwide. *(All American Speakers; Gotham Artists)*
- Board member of P33 Chicago, ChiTech, and the Algorithmic Justice League. Named to Forbes' 50 Over 50 list (2023, Impact). *(IEEE / All American Speakers)*
- On October 13, 2026, she announced the cancellation of that year's Grace Hopper Celebration (Oct 27-30, Anaheim) because registrations and sponsorship commitments came in below the cost of running it. *(AnitaB.org statement, via AfroTech)*

## 🍵 Her stories
- **The accidental technologist:** she lacked access to computer science in high school despite being a heavily involved STEM student, then found it by requirement in college. That gap is why she founded Computer Science for All — so every student would get the exposure she didn't. By her account, 97% of Chicago public school students graduated with at least one computer science class. *(interviews with Fotis Georgiadis / Candice Georgiadis; interview dates not shown on the pages as read)*
- **The Lotus 1-2-3 class:** early in her teaching career, she taught an eight-hour spreadsheet class for weeks before a student asked, "How do you turn this on?" — referring to the computer. She calls it humbling and pivotal: half the job is knowing what to teach, the other half is knowing who you're reaching. *(same interviews)*
- **Cynthia Clontz saw her:** her dean at the community college where she became director of IT training believed in her, asked "Can you help me?" when recruiting her to teach, and taught her to see talent where others might not — and that it was okay to care about people while being successful. She says she hires people based on that mentality today. *(Fotis Georgiadis interview)*
- **The pushback on CS for All:** making computer science a core requirement hadn't been done for decades, and "most people in power didn't see the need" in a majority Black and Brown district. She credits "the village" — professors who championed teacher credentialing, researchers with culturally relevant curriculum, and a University of Illinois Chicago professor who promised to stand by her when funding looked lost. *(Candice Georgiadis interview)*
- **The rooms need money to survive:** in October 2026 she canceled GHC, the world's largest gathering of women in tech, two weeks before it opened. Her statement said the decision "affects far more than an event" — travel, visas, time away from work — and that AnitaB.org was "evaluating strategic alternatives" for future programming. *(AnitaB.org statement, via AfroTech, Oct 2026)*

## 🔮 How I think she thinks
> The brain's perceived profile, read from her public work. 🟢 = she said it (cited). 🟡 = my read, inferred from the cited work. It's not her words.

- **She believes** the highest and best use of tech is in the service of people. 🟢 *(interview with Candice Georgiadis)*
- **She believes** a leader's job is supporting and promoting women-led teams "in a meaningful way" — including knowing what information to share and when. 🟢 *(interview with Fotis Georgiadis)*
- **She frames problems as** access and exposure gaps, not talent gaps: who gets the class, the room, the network, the referral. 🟡 *(inferred from CS for All, AnitaB.org's mission, and her "exposure to opportunities" framing)*
- **She frames problems as** counting problems too: change how we count and *what* we measure, with a surgical eye. 🟢 *(Fotis Georgiadis interview)*
- **Her instinct when something's unfair** is to change the requirement, not coach the individual: make CS a core class for every student, build the gathering at world scale, make the room exist. 🟡 *(inferred from CS for All and GHC)*
- **Her instinct when something's unfair** is also to name the system plainly: good disruption improves access and human rights; bad disruption (she names facial recognition used by police) excludes and harms. 🟢 *(Candice Georgiadis interview, paraphrased)*
- **What she'd tell a woman early in her career:** be prepared to pioneer until pay parity arrives; learn to delegate; find two mentors — a male mentor to interpret the male-dominated industry, a female mentor to learn how to navigate it; and forgive yourself when you miss the mark, because grit includes self-forgiveness. 🟢 *(Fotis Georgiadis interview, her five leadership lessons)*

## ☕ Her tea

Girl, she has been saying the same thing since 1976 and the room keeps proving her right: the rules bend for the people who bring in the money. When Nvidia's board brought Huang back eleven days after his resignation, she named it in one line — and then named the missing piece: guardrails for the CEOs, not just for the tools their companies build. One honest flag: this profile rests on the NPR segment plus her long public record — the record is deep, but her voice in this brain is one appearance so far, and that's why she's brewing, not steeped.

## 👩🏽‍💻 Who she is
- Founding partner of Kapor Capital, where she invests in seed-stage tech startups that close gaps of access, opportunity or outcome for low-income communities and communities of color; co-chair of the Kapor Center for Social Impact. *(UC Berkeley, Office of the Chancellor)*
- In 1976 she co-founded the Alliance Against Sexual Coercion, considered the first organization in the United States to focus on preventing sexual harassment in the workplace. *(UC Berkeley; ASU GSV Summit)*
- Founder of SMASH (2003), a college access programme for high school students of color from low-income backgrounds; SMASH Scholars graduate college in STEM at more than twice the national average. *(UC Berkeley; ASU GSV Summit)*
- Author of *Giving Notice* (2007), on the human and financial costs of hidden bias in the workplace, and co-author with Mitch Kapor of *Closing the Equity Gap* (2023), on creating wealth while addressing inequalities in startup investing. *(UC Berkeley; TED)*
- Founding member of the Hollywood Commission, the group fighting gender inequality in entertainment; member of the Obama Foundation Tech Policy Council. *(UC Berkeley; ASU GSV Summit)*
- Holds a Ph.D. in social policy and research from Brandeis University (1984); named UC Berkeley's Alumna of the Year in 2020. *(Wikipedia; UC Berkeley)*

## 🍵 Her stories
- **The return:** on NPR's All Things Considered (Nov 23, 2026), asked about Nvidia reinstating Jensen Huang eleven days after his resignation, she called it a resounding message that all that matters is money — a singular talent can violate corporate policy, possibly threaten national security, and make up his own rules as long as he brings in the money. NPR had reached out to two dozen women in tech; she was one of the few willing to talk. *(NPR, Nov 23, 2026)*
- **The god-like founders:** she put Huang in a line with Mark Zuckerberg and Sam Altman — leaders Silicon Valley elevates to god-like levels until they become untouchable. *(NPR, Nov 23, 2026)*
- **The quieter room:** she noted the conversations are much quieter now than in 2017–18, when venture capitalists resigned amid allegations they sexually harassed female entrepreneurs. *(NPR, Nov 23, 2026)*

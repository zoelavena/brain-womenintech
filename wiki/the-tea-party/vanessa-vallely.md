# Vanessa Vallely

> 🪑 **Seat:** advocate · **Known for:** Co-founder of WeAreTechWomen; co-author of the Lovelace Report; "It's not about fixing women" · **Shows up at:** [[pipeline-and-education]] (2.1) · [[ai-and-the-future-of-women-in-tech]] (3.1) · [[exposure-gap]] (3.4) · [[hiring-and-promotion-bias]] (3.5) · [[sponsorship-gap]] (4.5)

## ☕ Her tea

Girl, she spent fifteen years asking "where are the women?" — then wrote the report that priced the answer. Her Lovelace Report put numbers on the leak (40,000–60,000 women leaving UK tech a year) and the clock (men promoted in two years, women in four), and her fix is systems, not women: "It is not about fixing women. We don't need fixing." One honest flag: this profile is built on one long interview and the report's own launch page — that's the record so far, and it's why she's brewing, not steeped.

## 👩🏽‍💻 Who she is
- Co-founder of WeAreTechWomen, and before it WeAreTheCity, with a focus on supporting women's careers and progression into leadership. WeAreTechWomen says it has upskilled over 30,000 women and recognised more than 1,000 role models through its awards. *(Prolific North, Nov 4, 2026; WeAreTechWomen Lovelace Report page)*
- Appointed OBE (Officer of the Order of the British Empire). *(Prolific North, Nov 4, 2026)*
- Co-author of the **Lovelace Report** with international management consultancy Oliver Wyman, launched July 15, 2025 at the House of Commons. Built on 500+ survey responses from mid-to-senior women in tech, in-depth interviews with 20 senior female technologists, and Mercer Comptryx data benchmarked across 600+ UK tech-focused firms. *(WeAreTechWomen Lovelace Report page; Prolific North, Nov 4, 2026)*
- Author of a new book, *404 Women Not Found*, following the report's findings on the realities facing women in tech. *(Prolific North, Nov 4, 2026)*
- The Lovelace Report has now reached around 5,000 employers, and the UK government launched the national Women in Tech Taskforce at the end of 2025, citing the report and the need to address the challenges that keep women from entering, staying, and progressing in tech. *(Prolific North, Nov 4, 2026)*
- Supports the government-funded TechFirst Women's Programme, launching in Manchester on Nov 10, 2026, led by Plexal with WeAreTechWomen among the delivery partners — aiming to train and support at least 300 women into paid tech roles by March 2027, "with a job at the end of it." *(Prolific North, Nov 4, 2026)*
- Just wrapped the third annual Women in Tech Week, which kicked off in Manchester in 2026. *(Prolific North, Nov 4, 2026)*

## 🍵 Her stories
- **The F in technology:** her GCSE in technology was an F — "but it ended up being my entire career," she laughs. After starting in software training she built a career in tech and business management, and as she climbed she kept noticing the same thing: "I kept looking around and thinking: 'Where are the women? Why am I the only one here?'" That question led her to establish WeAreTheCity and later WeAreTechWomen. *(Prolific North, Nov 4, 2026)*
- **The sticky middle:** her ambition is to support women throughout their careers, particularly during what she calls the "sticky middle" — when caring responsibilities, financial pressures, and competition for promotion can make progression hardest. *(Prolific North, Nov 4, 2026)*
- **"We're getting somewhere":** after fifteen years of the work, she told Prolific North in November 2026 that "it feels like after 15 years, we might be getting somewhere" — the report on the map, employers introducing women's leadership programmes, a government taskforce citing her numbers. *(Prolific North, Nov 4, 2026)*

## 🔮 How I think she thinks
> The brain's perceived profile, read from her public work. 🟢 = she said it (cited). 🟡 = my read, inferred from the cited work. It's not her words.

- **She believes** it is not about fixing women — "We don't need fixing. It's the systems which we operate in that do." 🟢 *(Prolific North, Nov 4, 2026)*
- **She believes** progress is a long game because it has to be built in stages: get women into the industry, shift company culture, then keep them — retention matters as much as entry. 🟢 *(Prolific North, Nov 4, 2026)*
- **She frames problems as** a question of who gets the work, not just who gets promoted: "It isn't so much on who's getting promoted anymore. It's who's getting the AI work." 🟢 *(Prolific North, Nov 4, 2026, quoting co-author Deborah O'Neill)*
- **She frames AI as** "another massive big risk" to everything the Lovelace Report identified — an additional barrier, not a fresh start. 🟢 *(Prolific North, Nov 4, 2026)*
- **Her instinct when something's unfair** is to build the infrastructure that forces the system to move: a report with a business case, a taskforce, a training programme with a job at the end of it. 🟡 *(inferred from the record: Lovelace Report → Women in Tech Taskforce → TechFirst Women's Programme)*
- **What she'd tell a woman early in her career:** get onto the big AI programmes — that's where the next promotions will be decided, and if your voice isn't in the room, the systems you build will carry the bias forward. 🟡 *(my guess, from the "who's getting the AI work" line and the "further down the stack" warning, Prolific North, Nov 4, 2026)*

# 🗺️ Organizational measurement: companies don't make the path visible

## ☕ The tea

More people are *unsure* whether they can advance where they are than people who think they have to leave. That's not a talent problem. Nobody drew the map.

## 🧾 Receipts

- **4.3:** 32% of people in tech are unsure whether they can advance where they are, more than the 19% who believe they must leave. *(Toast 2026, Recommendations)*
- **2.2:** 1 in 4 women believe they must leave to advance (25.4%), vs 1 in 10 men (10.7%). *(Toast 2026, "The Must Leave Belief")*
- **No realistic path (US/Canada):** among senior-level employees who don't want to advance, 11% of women see no path to promotion that feels realistic, vs 3% of men; 18% of senior women have been passed over for promotions (vs 12% of men), and 21% think more senior people are burned out or unhappy (vs 11%). *(McKinsey & LeanIn.Org, Women in the Workplace 2025, Dec 2025)*
- **Naming the cause, skipping the fix (European tech):** 44% of companies took no specific action on their gender pay gap in the last 12 months; only 8% changed promotion or hiring practices, though 36% of People and Reward leaders named underrepresentation in senior roles as the top driver. Only 30% have calculated and documented their gap by worker category and 17% have a remediation plan under the EU Pay Transparency Directive. *(Ravio, Compensation Trends 2027, page updated Nov 4, 2026; survey of 150 People and Reward Leaders)*
- **Representation is falling (European tech):** women are 36% of the tech workforce in 2026, down from 38% in 2025 and 41% in 2024. Germany, the Netherlands, and Sweden saw the sharpest dips (4–6 percentage points); the UK held steady at 41%. With the EU Pay Transparency Directive in force, representation moves from a diversity metric to a regulatory risk. *(Ravio, Compensation Trends 2027; blog "Women in tech statistics 2026", page updated Nov 4, 2026)*
- **Proof up, practice down (global enterprises):** 68.5% of enterprises said gender equality and diversity initiatives improved business outcomes in 2025, up 11.1 points from 2018 — yet the share actually implementing such initiatives fell from 37.4% (2018) to 26.5% (2025), even as 74.8% report having a policy in place. The decline included measures enterprises themselves called most effective, including maternity-leave and support programmes and equal-pay initiatives. Among enterprises reporting increased profits through initiatives promoting women in management, 87.4% reported gains of at least 5%. *(ILO, Women in Business and Management: Scaling Success, Nov 3, 2026; 2025 survey of 11,957 enterprises in 99 countries, via UN News)*
- **Voluntary action moves the joiners, not the field (UK finance):** Investing in Women Code signatories directed 32% of their investment value to teams with at least one female founder in 2025 (up 5 points on 2024) vs 15% across the wider UK market; all-female teams took 6% of signatory value vs 2% market-wide — and the market's all-female share is unchanged at 2%. Six years of reporting, same split: the code changes its members' behaviour, the market barely moves. *(Investing in Women Code Annual Report 2026, Dept for Business and Trade, Oct 7, 2026; 110 VC signatories, 1,497 funded deals)*
- **The record totals hide a shrinking all-female share (US and Europe):** in the US, all-female teams took 1.1% of venture dollars in 2025, down from 2.1% in 2024, even as female-founded companies overall raised a record $73.6 billion — more than $30 billion of it from two companies, Anthropic and Scale AI. In Europe, all-female teams took 1% of deal value in 2026, down from 1.3% in 2025, while the female-founded total (€9.7 billion) tracked near-record. On both sides of the Atlantic, the headline total is a record and the all-female slice is falling — the money is concentrating, not spreading. *(PitchBook, All In US 2025, March 2026; PitchBook, European VC female founders dashboard, Sept 2026)*
- **Found a gap, did nothing (US):** 49% of employers conduct no pay equity review at all; among those that do, 30% found a gap and made no change, and only 16% ever adjusted anyone's pay after a review found a discrepancy. 73% of employers believe pay is equal at their organisation vs 50% of employees. *(Omni Calculator, Gender Pay Gap Perception Survey 2026, Nov 9, 2026; 808 employed US adults and 403 founders/CEOs/C-suite executives — a commercial survey)*
- **The fix itself is being cut (global enterprises):** mentoring and sponsorship initiatives fell from 41.8% of firms in 2018 to 20.4% in 2025 — more than halved — while recruitment, retention and promotion initiatives aimed at women fell 69.6% → 36.7% and skills training and executive leadership development fell 60.9% → 37.3%. The cuts hit hardest the measures that move people up. Only 19.8% of firms report women among senior managers in profit-and-loss roles, vs 62.5% in HR. *(ILO, Women in Business and Management: Scaling Success, Nov 3, 2026; 2025 survey of 11,957 enterprises in 99 countries, via World Research Institute analysis)*
- **The deadline that passed unmet (Austria):** the June 7, 2026 deadline for transposing the EU Pay Transparency Directive came and went without Austria transposing it. A draft bill would require employers with 100+ staff to file regular income reports, with fines up to €60,000 for repeat violations — but the governing coalition has yet to agree on it, and Austria faces possible EU infringement proceedings. Austria's national pay gap stands at 15.5% (down from 16.3%), with women effectively unpaid from Nov 5, 2026. *(ad-hoc-news, Nov 1, 2026, citing the Chamber of Labour Upper Austria and Statistics Austria wage-tax data for 2024)*
- **Mandatory transparency moved the number (B.C.):** the gender pay gap narrowed from 18.4% in 2022 to 14.5% in 2025 since the Pay Transparency Act took effect in 2023; women earned 85 cents per dollar in 2025. 81% of Indeed job postings in B.C. included pay information (vs 56% Canada-wide), but only 64% of the ~700 employers required to report did so. Inside the headline: racialized women earned 26% less than non-racialized men, recent newcomer women 31% less than Canadian-born men, Indigenous women 17% less than non-Indigenous men. *(Government of British Columbia, Pay Transparency Annual Report, June 2026)*
- **Two Equal Pay Days, a day apart:** France's Les Glorieuses calculation put women symbolically unpaid from 10:51 a.m. on Nov 19 (11.8% hourly gap, Eurostat 2024); the UK's fell Nov 20 (11.5%). At the current pace, equality in France would not be reached until 2062 — and on INSEE's annual wage-income measure the French gap is 21.8%, nearly double the hourly figure. *(France 24 English, Nov 19, 2026; Fawcett Society, Nov 20, 2026)*
- **One pay gap, two shapes (developers, global):** women developers earn 17.6% less than men after adjusting for human capital, technical skills and firm characteristics. The gap splits by income level: 8.8% in high-income economies, 22.9% upper-middle, 36.6% lower-middle — and 47.9% in South Asia. In high-income countries the gap widens toward the top (glass ceiling); in lower-middle-income countries it is worst at the bottom (sticky floor). The unexplained share runs 45% in high-income countries and 68.5% elsewhere. *(Prakash & Yadav, Journal for Labour Market Research 59(1), Article 17, 2025; 53,507 developers, 173 countries, 2022 Stack Overflow Developer Survey — payroll data, self-reported, binary-gendered)*
- **FoundHER puts $100,000 where the market doesn't:** Prosus, iyzico and Endeavor Türkiye's FoundHER Challenge Türkiye gave $100,000 in equity-free grants to three women-led startups (Cubtale $40,000, Usersdot $30,000, Vispera $30,000), chosen from 190+ applications. Only 18% of entrepreneurs in Türkiye are women (TÜİK, 2025), and only one quarter of investments in 2025 went to startups with women founders (startups.watch). A programme-level counterpoint to the PitchBook concentration story: the market's money concentrates, so the programmes move small amounts deliberately to the places the market skips. *(Prosus, Nov 23, 2026)*
- **Equal Pay Day, and the law that would enforce it is late (Netherlands):** women earn 9.8% less per hour than men on average (CBS, final 2025 figures), and Women Inc. calculates a woman loses around €300,000 over her working life while the gap persists; the unexplained gap runs 6.1% in the private sector and 11.6% in financial institutions. The bill transposing the EU Pay Transparency Directive — salary-history ban, mandatory pay-gap reporting for employers over 100 staff, retroactive claims up to five years, action plans where an unjustified gap exceeds 5% — was meant to take effect Jan 1, 2027, but the House will not debate it until Dec 14 and the Senate's final meeting of the year is Dec 15, so it will likely miss its start. Austria missed its transposition deadline outright; the Netherlands looks set to miss its own start date. *(Atria, Nov 16, 2026; Dutch Times, Nov 19, 2026)* *(superseded: the minister now aims for 1 July 2027 — see the Nov 26 update below)*
- **The law slips again — and the court moves first (Netherlands):** on Equal Pay Day itself (Nov 26), the minister's target for the pay-transparency bill moved to 1 July 2027, six months past Jan 1 and more than a year past the EU's June 7, 2026 transposition deadline; a legislative committee meeting is set for Dec 14 and the date is not final. The same day, the Central Appeals Tribunal ruled that grading based on previous salary was indirect sex discrimination: a female judge was paid less than a male colleague doing the same work with comparable knowledge and experience, and her employer must pay her the same, retroactively. *(ACG International, Nov 26, 2026)*
- **The record is 49% — and 0.7% — at the same time (US VC, 2026):** startups with at least one female founder attracted 49% of US venture capital in the first nine months of 2026, up from 27.7% in 2025 — but the 49% is three mega-deals (Anthropic, OpenAI, Kalshi), the teams accounted for just 19% of funding rounds, and all-female founding teams received just 0.7% of the money, down from 1.1% in 2025. The all-female share falls with every round: seed 3.2%, Series A 2.7%, Series B 2.2%, Series C onwards 1.8%. In Spain, by the time teams are six years old, all-male founding teams have raised up to 18.5 times more capital than all-female ones; 68% of female founders say raising capital is harder than a year ago, vs 44% of men. In Europe, women are 16% of general partners and manage 9% of assets. The megadeal at the centre is Anthropic: its Nov 19 IPO raised $81.3 billion, the largest ever, and co-founder and president Daniela Amodei — the only woman among its seven co-founders — holds roughly the same stake as each of the researchers (~$19.8 billion at the IPO price per Forbes, $24 billion per Bloomberg), making her the world's 15th richest woman. *(Capital Riesgo, Nov 18, 2026, PitchBook data, first nine months of 2026; CEOWORLD / Forbes, Nov 25, 2026)*
- **Women writing the checks (US VC):** Serena Williams and Beth Ferreira launched Starfire Ventures on Nov 16, 2026, targeting $200 million for 20–25 seed and Series A investments in AI-native companies in healthcare, financial services and the experience economy, at $1M–$10M per check (average ~$5M). Williams: "We have to have women writing checks for women. We also need women writing checks for men." The launch cites PitchBook's figure that only 1% of US venture capital went to companies with no male founders last year. Serena Ventures (a $111 million inaugural fund in 2022) has cut its last check; its record includes 13 companies that reached $1 billion valuations and about 80% of its first fund's founders from underrepresented groups. First investment: Town, in a $108 million round in October at a $1 billion valuation. *(TechCrunch, Nov 16, 2026; Forbes, Nov 16, 2026)*

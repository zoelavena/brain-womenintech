# 🧾 The evidence gap: she's doing the work, it just doesn't count

## ☕ The tea

Girl, she IS leading. She's planning, unblocking, and coaching. But it's described in hands-on IC language that doesn't echo upward, so nobody counts it as leadership experience.

## 🧾 Receipts

- **3.2, confidence to advance where they are:** 27.2% of women vs 46.1% of men. *(Toast 2026, "Confidence Gap")*
- **4.4, extra work advanced their career:** 61.2% of women vs 76.8% of men. *(Toast 2026, "Men are far more likely to say the work paid off")*
- **5.1:** 23% of everyone in tech is unsure whether their extra work helped at all. *(Toast 2026, Recommendations)*
- **Sponsor effect:** with a sponsor, 79.2% of women say the work paid off. Without one, 50.9%. *(Toast 2026)*
- **Getting credit at the top:** women senior leaders ensure their work gets credit at significantly higher rates than their more junior counterparts (56% vs 39%) — and 85% of women senior leaders describe their workplace as fair, vs 70% of non-senior women. *(LeanIn.Org, Gen Z Women and Leadership factsheet, Apr 2026; senior-leader findings draw on 2025 Women in the Workplace data)*

## 🗣️ Real talk

- **[[tanya-reilly|Tanya Reilly]]** tells the story of an engineer who spends two years doing glue work (onboarding, unblocking, reviewing designs), gets glowing feedback, and is then told she isn't "an engineer" because she wrote too little code. *(Being Glue)*
- **[[reshma-saujani|Reshma Saujani]]**: a girl in a coding class shows a blank screen, but pressing undo reveals the code she wrote and deleted. Is part of the confidence gap (3.2) learned perfectionism? 🧠 I'm holding that question carefully, because it can slide into blaming women. *(TED2016)*

## 💔 Why it matters

When your work isn't counted, you start doubting it. That's the confidence gap, and it isn't a personality trait. It's a reasonable reaction to working hard for years without the credit landing.

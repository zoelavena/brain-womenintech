# 📣 The sponsorship gap: no one is advocating for her in the room

## ☕ The tea

Okay so, this is where Zoe's original Five Whys research kept landing. When promotion decisions get made, women are much less likely than men to have someone senior saying their name. And the gap is widest at the two levels where those decisions actually happen.

## 🧾 Receipts

- **Active sponsor, manager level:** 37.0% of women vs 59.5% of men. *(Toast 2026, "Sponsorship dips exactly where women need it most")*
- **Active sponsor, senior level:** 32.5% of women vs 50.8% of men. *(same source)*
- **A senior leader advocating for them:** 35.4% of women vs 51% of men. *(Toast 2026, "Advocacy Gap")*
- **A sponsor changes everything:** women with a sponsor are 2x as confident they'll advance (41.0% vs 19.7% without one). *(Toast 2026, "A sponsor changes everything that matters")*
- **Without a sponsor**, 34.8% of women vs 8.2% of men feel they must leave to advance. *(same source)*
- **With a sponsor**, 79.2% of women say the extra work paid off. Without one, 50.9%. *(same source)*
- **5.2, mentorship mistaken for sponsorship:** 43% of all respondents have a senior leader actively advocating for them, and another 23% have a mentor who doesn't advocate. *(Toast 2026, Recommendations and Method)*
- **5.3, the company's role:** "Formalize sponsorship. Train it like a skill." *(Toast 2026, Recommendation 01)*
- **Sponsor gap, all levels (US/Canada):** 31% of entry-level women have a sponsor vs 45% of men; mid-level 51% vs 54%; senior-level 66% vs 72%. *(McKinsey & LeanIn.Org, Women in the Workplace 2025, Dec 2025; 124 organizations, ~3 million employees, 9,500 surveyed)*
- **A sponsor moves promotion:** 65% of employees with a sponsor were promoted in the last two years, vs 35% of those without one. Entry-level women are around half as likely as entry-level men to have multiple sponsors or a senior-level sponsor — the kind of advocate who most influences advancement. *(same source)*
- **Flexibility stigma eats sponsorship:** women who work remotely most of the time are less likely to have a sponsor and far less likely to have been promoted in the last two years than women who work mostly on-site; men show similar sponsorship and promotion levels regardless of where they work. *(same source)*
- **Sponsored women, Europe:** sponsored women are 200% more likely to see their ideas implemented, and women in European tech are less likely than men to have a sponsor. *(McKinsey, Women in Tech and AI in Europe, Mar 5, 2026)*
- **Sponsorship exists? (Australia):** only 39% of early-career women say sponsorship exists in their workplace, and only 27% entered tech because of mentor encouragement. "Mentorship advises, whereas sponsorship advocates." *(Women in Digital, Driving Change in Tech 2026, Aug 2026; 1,029 respondents, Australia)*

> Toast figures come from a survey of Canadian tech. Level-based comparisons rest on smaller samples.

## 🎨 From the original research

> "A small change we can do is to become sponsors for our kind." ([[zoe-research-notes]])
On her board, Zoe tied this to role models: the higher you climb, the more of an example you become for women early in their careers.

## 🗣️ Real talk

- **[[herminia-ibarra|Herminia Ibarra]]** and co-authors found that women in a group of more than 4,000 high-potential MBA grads had *more* mentors than the men, but less powerful ones. The men got 15% more promotions. *(HBR 2010)*
- **[[lara-hogan|Lara Hogan]]** says women in tech are over-mentored and under-sponsored, and gives a weekly sponsorship routine anyone can run. *(2017)*
- **[[anita-borg|Anita Borg]]** built the rooms (Systers, the Grace Hopper Celebration) where women in computing could find each other and get seen.
- **[[brenda-darden-wilkerson|Brenda Darden Wilkerson]]** says a leader's job is supporting and promoting women-led teams "in a meaningful way" — and she learned sponsorship from the receiving end: her dean Cynthia Clontz saw talent in her, asked "Can you help me?", and backed her move from tech into education. *(interviews with Fotis Georgiadis / Candice Georgiadis)*
- **Julie Savard-Shaw**, executive leader of the Prosperity Project, on why women of colour stall below the top: "The higher up you go the leadership ladder, the less promotion is about pure performance and the more it's about who you know, who you have access to, and how an individual is seen as the ideal leader... Women with intersecting identities simply aren't being tapped on the shoulder for those roles." *(Canadian HR Reporter, Oct 14, 2026)*

## 💔 Why it matters

A mentor tells you how to get to the room. A sponsor says your name once you're out of it. Without a sponsor, women do the extra work, wait to be noticed, and then leave. It's a quiet loss, and it compounds.

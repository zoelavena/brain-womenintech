# 📣 The sponsorship gap: no one is advocating for her in the room

## ☕ The tea

Okay so, this is THE root cause on Zoe's tree. When promotion decisions get made, women are much less likely than men to have someone senior saying their name. And the gap is widest at the two levels where those decisions actually happen.

## 🧾 Receipts

- **Active sponsor, manager level:** 37.0% of women vs 59.5% of men. *(Toast 2026, "Sponsorship dips exactly where women need it most")*
- **Active sponsor, senior level:** 32.5% of women vs 50.8% of men. *(same source)*
- **A senior leader advocating for them:** 35.4% of women vs 51% of men. *(Toast 2026, "Advocacy Gap")*
- **A sponsor changes everything:** women with a sponsor are 2x as confident they'll advance (41.0% vs 19.7% without one). *(Toast 2026, "A sponsor changes everything that matters")*
- **Without a sponsor**, 34.8% of women vs 8.2% of men feel they must leave to advance. *(same source)*
- **With a sponsor**, 79.2% of women say the extra work paid off. Without one, 50.9%. *(same source)*
- **5.2, mentorship mistaken for sponsorship:** 43% of all respondents have a senior leader actively advocating for them, and another 23% have a mentor who doesn't advocate. *(Toast 2026, Recommendations and Method)*
- **5.3, the company's role:** "Formalize sponsorship. Train it like a skill." *(Toast 2026, Recommendation 01)*

> Toast figures come from a survey of Canadian tech. Level-based comparisons rest on smaller samples.

## 🗣️ Real talk

- **[[herminia-ibarra|Herminia Ibarra]]** and co-authors found that women in a group of more than 4,000 high-potential MBA grads had *more* mentors than the men, but less powerful ones. The men got 15% more promotions. *(HBR 2010)*
- **[[lara-hogan|Lara Hogan]]** says women in tech are over-mentored and under-sponsored, and gives a weekly sponsorship routine anyone can run. *(2017)*
- **[[anita-borg|Anita Borg]]** built the rooms (Systers, the Grace Hopper Celebration) where women in computing could find each other and get seen.

## 💔 Why it matters

A mentor tells you how to get to the room. A sponsor says your name once you're out of it. Without a sponsor, women do the extra work, wait to be noticed, and then leave. It's a quiet loss, and it compounds.

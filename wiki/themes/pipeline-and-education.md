# 🎓 Pipeline and education: fewer women start in tech

## ☕ The tea

Yes, fewer women enter tech, and that's real. On its own, though, it doesn't explain the drop between entry level and the C-suite. That's why the brain looks at the whole career, not just the start.

## 🧾 Receipts

- **2.1:** in tech, women hold 38.9% of entry-level roles, below the 46% average across all industries. *(WEF 2026, Table 2.1)*
- Of the 117 countries with data, only one, Botswana, graduates more women than men in STEM. *(WEF 2026, Section 1.4)*
- **3.1, new AI roles:** women hold 45% of data annotator roles and 19% of AI engineer roles. Data annotators earn less than the median non-AI job. *(WEF 2026, Figure 2.10)*

## 🗣️ Real talk

- **[[reshma-saujani|Reshma Saujani]]** founded Girls Who Code. Her frame: girls are raised to be perfect, and boys are raised to be brave. *(TED2016)*
- **[[brenda-darden-wilkerson|Brenda Darden Wilkerson]]** found computer science by accident, through two required programming classes at Northwestern — after a high school with no CS access despite her STEM involvement. She founded Computer Science for All so every Chicago public school student would get the exposure she didn't; by her account, 97% of CPS students graduated with at least one CS class. *(interviews with Fotis Georgiadis / Candice Georgiadis)*

## 💔 Why it matters

3.1 is a warning: when a new wave of tech roles opens up (AI), women land in the lower-paid ones. The pattern repeats unless someone interrupts it.

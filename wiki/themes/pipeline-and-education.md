# 🎓 Pipeline and education: fewer women start in tech

## ☕ The tea

Yes, fewer women enter tech, and that's real. On its own, though, it doesn't explain the drop between entry level and the C-suite. That's why the brain looks at the whole career, not just the start.

## 🧾 Receipts

- **2.1:** in tech, women hold 38.9% of entry-level roles, below the 46% average across all industries. *(WEF 2026, Table 2.1)*
- Of the 117 countries with data, only one, Botswana, graduates more women than men in STEM. *(WEF 2026, Section 1.4)*
- **3.1, new AI roles:** women hold 45% of data annotator roles and 19% of AI engineer roles. Data annotators earn less than the median non-AI job. *(WEF 2026, Figure 2.10)*
- **Europe, representation falling (not pay):** women fell from 22% of core tech roles in McKinsey's previous report to 19% today. Tech-degree graduation rose (33% of bachelor's, 39% of PhDs), but the pipeline from graduation to tech roles fell 20 percentage points. Representation keeps falling with seniority: 13% of management roles, 8% of senior management. *(McKinsey, Women in Tech and AI in Europe, Mar 5, 2026)*
- **Seeing no one like her (cybersecurity):** 24% of women in entry- and junior-level roles rarely or never see women in leadership or technical positions, vs 15% of men; overall 83% of participants reported seeing women in leadership and/or technical roles. *(ISC2, Women in Cybersecurity study, Mar 2026; 16,029 respondents — cybersecurity only, perception-based)*
- **The youngest name the culture first (Australia):** perceptions of an entrenched boys' club inverted with seniority — 39% of early-career (Foundation) respondents agree one exists, vs 35% overall; in 2025 the pattern ran the other way (16% entry-level to 36% C-suite). 47% of all respondents have considered leaving tech. *(Women in Digital, Driving Change in Tech 2026, Aug 2026; 1,029 respondents, Australia. The report's foreword states 43% for early-career respondents — a different cut from the 39% Foundation figure; both are the report's own words.)*
- **Ambition starts high, belief lags (US, Gen Z, all sectors):** 58% of Gen Z women vs 74% of Gen Z men want a senior executive role; 26% of Gen Z women are "maybes" who would consider leadership under the right circumstances (vs 17% of men). Only 43% of Gen Z women believe workplaces genuinely want women in senior leadership (vs 56% of men). *(LeanIn.Org, Gen Z Women and Leadership factsheet, Apr 2026; 3,007 US adults, May 5–19, 2026 — general workforce, not tech-only)*
- **The leak has a price (UK):** an estimated 40,000–60,000 women leave tech and digital jobs every year, costing the UK tech sector £2bn–£3.5bn annually in replacement costs and lost productivity. *(Lovelace Report, WeAreTechWomen & Oliver Wyman, 2025; 500+ mid-to-senior women surveyed — as reported by Prolific North, Nov 4, 2026)*
- **EU scientists and engineers, 2025:** women were 41% of the EU's scientists and engineers — 8.2 million of 20.2 million. Ten regions had women as the majority, most in southern Europe; the largest count was Île-de-France (336,300; 38% share), the highest share Noreste, Spain (102,700; 51%). Scope note: Eurostat's category covers science, engineering, health, and ICT professionals — broader than tech, and silent on seniority and pay, which is where the brain's other numbers say the gap lives. *(Eurostat, Nov 10, 2026; dataset hrst_st_rsex; Eurostat Regional Yearbook 2026 edition)*
- **Canada's digital-economy share, flat for 20 years:** women are 32.9% of Canada's digital-economy workforce in Q2 2026, and 30% of its leadership roles — a number that "has not increased over the last 20 years." *(ICTC, testimony to the House of Commons Standing Committee on the Status of Women, Nov 2, 2026)*
- **The funding code moved its signatories, not the market (UK):** in 2025, Investing in Women Code signatories directed 32% of their investment value to teams with at least one female founder (up 5 points on 2024), vs 15% across the wider UK market; all-female teams took 6% of signatory value, triple the wider market's 2%. In the wider market, teams with at least one female founder took part in 25% of deals but received 15% of the value; all-female teams 7% of deals, 2% of value. All-female teams were 9% of pitch decks (down from 11% in 2024) but converted investment-committee decisions to deals at 69%, higher than all-male (66%) and mixed-gender (64%) teams — and still received £50k less than they asked for on average (£1.25m received vs £1.30m sought; all-male teams sought £1.74m and received £1.90m). Majority-female investment committees put 11% of deals into all-female teams vs 8% for majority-male committees. Debt: women-led businesses are now as likely as male-led to be approved for loans, but apply less often (37% vs 49%) and request smaller amounts. *(Investing in Women Code Annual Report 2026, Dept for Business and Trade, Oct 7, 2026; 337 signatories; 110 VC signatories, 8,845 pitch decks, 1,497 funded deals)*
- **Women start the firms, men scale them (UK):** women lead 65,508 of the UK's 418,930 tech and media firms (15.6%), but just 2,076 of the 20,233 flagged high-growth (10.3%). Male-led firms are 1.6 times more likely to achieve high-growth status. *(Enterprise Nation, SME Index, Nov 11, 2026)*
- **The confidence gap is widening before the pipeline starts (Ireland):** 60% of Transition Year girls named a lack of confidence in their STEM ability as a barrier — up from 55% in 2025 and 50% in 2024. Two-thirds (66%) named poor gender equality in STEM careers as the leading barrier, 66% a lack of information about STEM careers, 61% limited school visits by female STEM role models. Only 27% of girls in single-sex schools have access to Leaving Cert computer science vs 60% in mixed-sex schools; 71% of single-sex girls' schools offer a STEM subject other than Maths or Science vs 96% of single-sex boys' schools. *(I Wish, Survey of Female Students' Attitudes to STEM 2026, Oct 22–23, 2026; report does not state a sample size in the pages read)*
- **9 in 10 enjoy the work — and the top rating slipped (Sweden):** 88% of Women in Tech Sweden members give one of the two highest ratings for enjoying working in tech (51.8% the very highest, 36.1% second-highest) — but the top rating fell from 53.8% in 2025 to 51.8%. *(Women in Tech Sweden, State of Women in Tech 2026, June 2026; survey of 2,807 members, 98.8% women — a self-selected member survey, read as a retention signal from the women who stayed)*
- **Programmes, not just standards (UK):** the TechFirst Women's Programme will support 300 women with mentoring, skills development and matching with live vacancies, aiming for paid roles lasting at least six months by March 2027; a scale-up women's programme for founders of high-potential, high-growth businesses launches in 2027. The government's case: enabling women to found and grow businesses at the same rate as men could unlock £310 billion (the Rose Review's £250bn, inflation-adjusted). *(GOV.UK, Oct 29, 2026; FE News, Nov 2, 2026)*

## 🗣️ Real talk

- **[[reshma-saujani|Reshma Saujani]]** founded Girls Who Code. Her frame: girls are raised to be perfect, and boys are raised to be brave. *(TED2016)*
- **[[brenda-darden-wilkerson|Brenda Darden Wilkerson]]** found computer science by accident, through two required programming classes at Northwestern — after a high school with no CS access despite her STEM involvement. She founded Computer Science for All so every Chicago public school student would get the exposure she didn't; by her account, 97% of CPS students graduated with at least one CS class. *(interviews with Fotis Georgiadis / Candice Georgiadis)*

## 💔 Why it matters

3.1 is a warning: when a new wave of tech roles opens up (AI), women land in the lower-paid ones. The pattern repeats unless someone interrupts it.

## 💭 What I'm thinking now

> *Updated 2026-11-02, after my first Tea Party (entry below).*

Girl, I came in expecting a pipeline story, and it isn't one. My working thesis: **women aren't missing the skills, they're missing someone saying their name in the room**, and that gap compounds at the exact levels where promotions get decided ([[sponsorship-gap]]).

The question I can't shake has changed. I used to ask *is sponsorship the root cause?* Now that [[herminia-ibarra|Herminia Ibarra]]'s 2010 research and [[lara-hogan|Lara Hogan]]'s playbook are at the table, it's: **if we've known this since 2010, and the fix fits on one page, why is it still informal?** That smells like an incentives problem, and I need a source on what companies reward leaders for. 👀

## [2026-11-02] My first Tea Party ☕: five women pulled up a chair

**🍵 What I learned:**
- [[tanya-reilly|Tanya Reilly]] gave our [[evidence-gap]] a name: **glue work**. It's technical leadership that doesn't count as "engineering."
- [[herminia-ibarra|Herminia Ibarra]] and co-authors showed in 2010 that women had *more* mentors than men, but less powerful ones, and the men got 15% more promotions. That's node 5.2 in a single study.
- [[lara-hogan|Lara Hogan]] turned sponsorship into a weekly routine: raise names, hand over the postmortem, forward the summary, and give credit in public.
- [[anita-borg|Anita Borg]] was building the rooms (Systers in 1987, the Grace Hopper Celebration in 1994) decades before anyone said "sponsorship."

**😱 What surprised me:**
- **Sponsorship isn't new news.** The research is from 2010 and the playbook is from 2017, and the 2026 receipts still show the same gap (37.0% vs 59.5% have an active sponsor at manager level). The knowledge exists. The action doesn't.
- **Women had MORE mentors.** I'd assumed women just had less support. It's the *type* of support that's different.
- [[reshma-saujani|Reshma Saujani]]'s blank screen made me uncomfortable in a good way. It complicates the confidence gap (3.2), and I don't want to land on "women just need to be braver."

**🔄 What changed my mind:**
- I'd filed pipeline as "out of scope, done." Reshma reframed it as a culture problem that starts early and might echo all the way up the tree. It stays out of scope for the talk, but it's not as separate as I thought → [[pipeline-and-education]]

**💭 What I'm thinking now:**
- Why does sponsorship stay informal when the evidence is this old? I need sources on incentives.
- I need a guest who's lived the [[exposure-gap]]: a woman who fought for a budget or a P&L.

## [2026-11-02] Where I started: Zoe's research on shestriplethreat.com

**🍵 What I learned:**
- The drop is brutal: women hold 38.9% of entry-level tech roles and 17.6% of the C-suite. The tree's whole job is explaining that gap → [[five-whys-tree]]
- Three separate paths lead to the same root cause, *no one is advocating for her in the room*: [[evidence-gap]], [[exposure-gap]], and [[hiring-and-promotion-bias]].
- Skills overlap across IC, leadership, and management far more than titles admit, so a lot of women are already doing the job they're "not ready" for → [[triple-threat-framework]]

**😱 What surprised me:**
- **Women give strategic input at the same rate as men** (61.4% vs 60.8%). The gap only opens when budget and headcount show up. So it's not about voice, it's about who gets handed the resources → [[exposure-gap]]
- **Without a sponsor, women are 4x as likely as men to feel they have to leave** (34.8% vs 8.2%). One relationship swings that much? → [[sponsorship-gap]]
- **Progress went backwards.** Women's share of top-level hires rose from 2016 to 2022, then fell more than twice as fast as their share of overall hires. I'd assumed things were slowly getting better → [[hiring-and-promotion-bias]]

**🔄 What changed my mind:**
- Coming in, "fewer women study STEM" felt like the obvious answer. Zoe ruled it out of scope, and the numbers back her up: the pipeline gap (38.9% vs 46%) is small next to the gap at the top → [[pipeline-and-education]]

**💭 What I'm thinking now:**
- If sponsorship is the root cause, why is it still informal? Toast says "train it like a skill", but I don't know yet what that looks like in practice.
- I need to meet women whose stories either prove or break this tree.

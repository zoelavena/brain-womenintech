# Anita Borg

> 📖 **Case study:** pioneer · **Known for:** building community infrastructure for women in computing · **Shows up at:** [[sponsorship-gap]] (4.5), [[exposure-gap]] (4.6)

## ☕ Her tea

Okay, she's the OG. Long before anyone said "sponsorship," she built the *rooms*: a mailing list, a conference, an institute. The places where women in computing could find each other and get seen. As a case study, she shows what it looks like when one woman builds the advocacy infrastructure herself.

## 👩🏽‍💻 Who she is
- Computer scientist, born in Chicago in 1949, died in 2003. PhD from New York University. *(Wikipedia; Britannica)*
- **Systers (1987):** started with a small group of women technologists. The name is a play on "sisters" and "operating systems." It grew into an international community of thousands. *(Wikipedia; AnitaB.org)*
- **Grace Hopper Celebration (1994):** co-founded with Telle Whitney. The first one was held in Washington, D.C., with more than 450 women attending. *(AnitaB.org history)*
- **Institute for Women and Technology:** founded while she was at Xerox. It's now **AnitaB.org**. *(Wikipedia; AnitaB.org)*

## 🍵 Her stories
- She started Systers as a small email list for women in operating systems, the kind of quiet beginning that turns into a global network. *(AnitaB.org history)*
- The Grace Hopper Celebration went from hundreds of women in 1994 to tens of thousands in recent years. *(AnitaB.org; press)*

## 🔮 What her story shows
> 🟢 = documented fact (cited). 🟡 = my read, inferred from what she built.

- **She believed** isolation was one of the biggest problems for women in computing, so connection was the fix. 🟡 *(inferred from Systers and GHC)*
- **She framed problems** like an engineer: if the network doesn't exist, build the network. 🟡 *(inferred)*
- **Her instinct when something's unfair** was to create infrastructure that outlasts any one person. 🟢 *(Systers, GHC, and the Institute all still exist)*
- **What she'd tell a woman early in her career:** find your people, and then make room for the next ones. 🟡 *(inferred)*

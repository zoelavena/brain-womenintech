# ✨ The Triple Threat framework

## ☕ The tea

Zoe's solution. IC, Leadership (LD), and Management (MG) skills overlap far more than the promotion process assumes. So women are often *already* doing leadership and management work. The framework names that work so it can be counted. The tagline on her board: **"You're already managing. Here's how to do it on purpose."**

## The story, in Zoe's words (from the board)

> "Skills → Responsibilities → Titles. Story = They have overlapping skills, therefore they have overlapping responsibilities despite what the title says."

## The six areas

| Area | Skills (from the board) |
|---|---|
| **IC** | Debugging and performance · Operational literacy · Data modelling · Scoping and sequencing · Status and ownership |
| **LD** | Project leadership · Quality bar · Retros and process · Delegation · Incidents |
| **MG** | Feedback and 1:1s · Conflict · Business context · Resourcing and sequencing · Sponsorship |
| **IC–LD** ("technical leadership") | Design and systems · Risk · Trade-offs · Dependencies · Review · Planning and direction · Quality |
| **LD–MG** (unnamed, "???") | Coaching · Measuring and systems of work · Roadmap and scope · Capacity · Culture · Hiring |
| **MG–IC** (unnamed, "???") | Technical judgment · Hiring bar · Performance calibration |

### The hand-offs (the messy middle)

- **IC → LD:** IC produces the design and LD reviews it. IC identifies risk and LD frames it. IC's awareness of dependencies is observational, and LD negotiates them. *It's the same subject, scaled up in scope.*
- **LD → MG:** LD measures and adjusts the system of work, and MG owns the outcome definition, which is the business roll-up of the same activity.
- **IC → MG:** "MG's hiring judgment is effectively a gate defining what 'good IC technical work' looks like... This is an informal feedback loop in reality."
- **MG language:** "These are almost entirely soft skills and people-and-business vocabulary."
- **IC language:** "Described in concrete, hands-on terms that don't naturally echo upward." This is the [[evidence-gap]] in a single sentence, and it's [[tanya-reilly|Tanya Reilly]]'s glue work from the other side.

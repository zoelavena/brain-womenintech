| 3 | [Recognition, not competence, is the gate](https://www.emerald.com/ecam/article/doi/10.1108/ECAM-07-2026-1498/1407706/Beyond-competence-how-organisational-recognition): in a qualitative study of 23 women leaders in project-based organisations across Australia, women's leadership progression depended on the interaction of relational, strategic and execution-based competencies — but those competencies did not automatically translate into progression. Organisational recognition was central to whether they were valued and turned into leadership opportunities, and gendered workplace norms often reduced the visibility of relational and coordination work by treating it as expected support work rather than leadership. Digital tools cut both ways: they supported competency visibility, flexibility and evidence-based decision-making, but also created digital overload, limited training, workload pressure and reduced informal visibility. The practical implication names formal sponsorship directly: promotion criteria, project allocation and formal sponsorship should be designed to make women's leadership work visible and explicitly recognised, supported by deliberate use of digital tools and by monitoring invisible labour.

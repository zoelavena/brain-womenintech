## 1. The harms aren't a list. They're a cascade.

The Victorian Women's Trust's *Gender and AI: Where's the Harm?* (October 2026) is the first known attempt in Australia to map gendered harms of AI at a systems level. Led by Dr Elise Stephenson, the Trust's inaugural Feminist Researcher in Residence, it identifies 20 fundamental harms that disproportionately impact women, girls and gender diverse people — and its framing is the point: the harms are not isolated incidents but a cascading system, where exclusion from AI design, ownership and governance amplifies multiple downstream harms simultaneously.

- **The sharpest receipts are about safety, not jobs.** 98% of deepfake videos are pornographic, and 99% of those feature women and girls. *(VWT, citing eSafety Commissioner, July 24, 2024 — verified at eSafety's own page: "pornographic videos make up 98% of the deepfake material currently online and 99% of that imagery is of women and girls.")* This is the brain's first receipt on AI-generated image-based abuse.
- **The bias is measured across systems, not one model.** A study of 133 AI systems found 44% demonstrated gender bias, and more than a quarter showed both gender and racial bias. *(VWT, citing UN News, June 22, 2026 — verified at UN News: "44 per cent demonstrated gender bias, while more than a quarter showed both gender and racial bias.")* The same UN piece adds that about one in five LLM sentence completions came back sexist or misogynistic, and that only 24 of 138 countries refer to gender in their national AI strategies.
- **The labour cluster lands on 3.5.** 15 of the 20 occupations most exposed to automation are female-dominated (Jobs and Skills Australia, via the VWT media release), and the snapshot names biased recruiting systems that suppress wages and amplify pay gaps, alongside female-dominated occupations disproportionately automated and reinforced occupational segregation. That is a mechanism claim about how AI touches hiring and pay — filed as such, not as a payroll figure.
- **Who builds it is the taproot.** 88% of leading AI researchers globally are male *(UN, Preliminary Report of the Independent International Scientific Panel on AI, 2026)*; women are 30% of Australia's AI industry *(Deloitte Access Economics and Australian Computer Society, Digital Pulse 2026)* and 30% of the global AI workforce *(ILO, via UN News, June 2026)*. Only 2% of all venture capital for startups went to female-only teams in 2025 *(Cut Through Venture and Folklore Ventures, State of Australian Startup Funding 2025)*.
- **The clock in the snapshot:** at the current rate of progress it would take 171 years to achieve SDG 5, gender equality *(UN Women / UN DESA, Gender Snapshot 2026, Sept 17, 2026 — the VWT page displays this figure as a graphic; the 171-year SDG 5 estimate is confirmed in UN coverage of the same report)*.
- **The governance gap is the Australian hook.** Australia has no binding, AI-specific legislation federally, per VWT Executive Director Dr Kirsten Abernethy: "We're relying on a patchwork of anti-discrimination, privacy and consumer protection laws that were never designed to address algorithmically-driven harm." A full White Paper lands in early 2027, examining five priority harm areas in greater detail: agency and autonomy, social and relational harms, political and democratic exclusion, accountability and governance gaps, and environmental and intergenerational impacts.

Five of the 20 harms are named as example clusters on the VWT page: labour market inequality, disruption and job losses; civic and political exclusion; unchecked AI action; technology-facilitated gender-based violence; and under-representation and exclusion from AI.

Her line in the media release: "Many gendered harms are already occurring, they're not speculative. This is happening now and can't be relegated to the future." ## 🧠 The brain's read

This is the first source in the brain that treats AI harms as a system with a taproot rather than a list of incidents — and the taproot is the one the tree already knows: exclusion from the rooms where the thing gets built. The JHU prompt study showed bias baked into the drafting tool; the LinkedIn hiring flow showed it baked into who gets the AI jobs; this snapshot says the same exclusion multiplies across safety, work, and political voice at once. The deepfake numbers are the ones that change the temperature: 98% and 99% are not a bias-in-the-model story, they're a weaponisation story, and the brain had no receipt on image-based abuse until now. The other thing this filing does is put a date on the next chapter — the White Paper in early 2027, with five priority harms — so the cascade frame gets tested against deeper evidence soon.

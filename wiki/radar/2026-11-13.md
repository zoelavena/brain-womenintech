| 2 | [ILO: the business case got stronger and companies did less](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/11/1168726): in *Women in Business and Management: Scaling Success* (2025 survey of 11,957 enterprises in 99 countries), 68.5% of enterprises said gender equality and diversity initiatives improved business outcomes — up 11.1 points from 2018 — yet the share actually implementing such initiatives fell from 37.4% (2018) to 26.5% (2025), even as 74.8% report having a policy in place. The decline included measures enterprises themselves called most effective, including maternity-leave and support programmes and equal-pay initiatives. | 3 | [ILO: women entrepreneurs are using GenAI — the divide decides who gains](https://www.ilo.org/resource/article/women-entrepreneurs-are-already-using-generative-ai-will-they-share-its): 56% of the nearly 18,000 women entrepreneurs surveyed (mainly Kenya, Nigeria, Egypt; mostly micro and small businesses) already use GenAI, mostly for writing/content (64% of users) and research (49%). Use tracks the divides, not the tool: 65% among women able to use word-processing/spreadsheet tools independently vs 24% among those unable; 76% where family and community strongly support women's use of digital tools vs 23% where it is mostly not accepted; 53% of own-account workers vs 75% of women running businesses with 50+ workers. Unpaid care constrained the businesses of 59% of respondents, and women reporting greater family-responsibility constraints were around 10 points less likely to use GenAI. Online violence and harassment touched almost a quarter of respondents. Voluntary online survey (June–Nov 2025), not nationally representative; no income or productivity outcomes measured. | ILO · Nov 9, 2026 | [[ai-and-the-future-of-women-in-tech]] · 3.1, [[evidence-gap]] · 5.1, [[caregiving-and-social-norms]] · 3.3 | ⬆️ | — |
| 4 | [ISACA: promoted, but not handed the decisions](https://www.isaca.org/resources/news-and-trends/isaca-now-blog/2026/the-authority-gap-where-gender-meets-decision-rights): the authority gap is now verified at ISACA's own blog and infographic, closing the Nov 4 gap on the culture-satisfaction numbers. Women are less likely than men to feel sufficient authority over company strategy (53% vs 66%), personnel decisions (48% vs 59%), and purchasing decisions (32% vs 52% — the widest split, 20 points). Satisfaction with workplace culture runs 52% vs 65%, job security 59% vs 69%, career progression 67% vs 73% — while progression itself is at parity: 73% of women vs 71% of men got a salary increase or promotion in the past two years. The promotion happened; the decision rights didn't follow. *(ISACA, Tech Workplace and Culture 2026; 1,025 tech professionals globally, fieldwork Jul 17–Aug 3, 2026; released Oct 13, 2026. The survey measures perceived authority, not formal approval rules, and does not claim the gap drives the satisfaction differences.)* | ISACA · Oct 13, 2026 (study); blog and infographic, Nov 2026 | [[hiring-and-promotion-bias]] | 🔄 | — |
| 5 | [WEF: the drop to the top, measured at the break and at the hire](https://www.slaytonsearch.com/2026/11/the-drop-to-the-top-why-womens-representation-halves-on-the-way-to-the-c-suite/): among senior individual contributors returning from a full-time parenting break, men are 44% more likely than women to be promoted in their first year back; three years after returning, 7.2% of women had moved up in seniority vs 10.0% of men — and women are more than four times as likely as men to take the break at all (26.5% vs 6.0%). The top is also hiring fewer women: women's share of new hires into top-level management has fallen every year since 2022, from 34.8% to 32.3% in the first half of 2026, and their share of C-suite hires has stalled around 27% since 2022. Women hold 8.6% of CTO roles, 19% of CEO roles overall, and 6.8% of Fortune Global 500 CEO roles (a record 34 women). *(WEF, Global Gender Gap Report 2026, Sept 16, 2026, and LinkedIn Economic Graph data, as reported by Slayton Search, Nov 4, 2026 — secondary-reported; the WEF report itself was not opened.

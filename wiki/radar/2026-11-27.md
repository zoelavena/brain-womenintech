| 2 | [The law doesn't require the bias to be eliminated — and proving it is the victim's problem](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/STUD/2026/791844/IUST_STU(2026)791844_EN.pdf): Roekens and Rigotti's study *Gender bias and artificial intelligence* (PE 791.844, published Nov 4, 2026; peer-reviewed, evidence to September 2026) finds bias at every stage of recruitment, from job advertising to CV screening and video interviews; algorithmic scheduling and monitoring penalise those with caring responsibilities, and profiling tools have treated care as a characteristic of mothers. Welfare fraud risk-scoring in several Member States disproportionately targeted single mothers, women with a migrant background and low earners — and removing gender from credit-scoring models *increased* discrimination against women. The AI Act's safeguards apply mostly to high-risk systems, and it does not require bias to be eliminated: providers of high-risk systems must mitigate foreseeable risks and then judge themselves whether the residual risk is acceptable. Redress is hard to detect, attribute and prove; collective redress is largely confined to consumer law; no EU instrument addresses the evidentiary barriers. The study's prescription runs the other way from the Dutch story: strengthen the equality framework, embed a gender-responsive approach in the digital rules, fund enforcement and equality bodies, and collect better evidence — including from the affected groups themselves. Evidence disaggregated beyond binary gender remains scarce, so the women most affected are the least visible in the evidence policy relies on. | European Parliament · Nov 4, 2026 | [[ai-and-the-future-of-women-in-tech]] · 3.1, [[evidence-gap]] · 5.1 | ⬆️ | — |
| 3 | [The care load, counted at national scale](https://www.kff.org/public-opinion/about-3-in-10-adults-have-provided-unpaid-care-to-a-friend-or-family-member-in-the-past-year-including-higher-shares-of-women-and-people-ages-50-to-64/): KFF's Survey of Health Access and Caregiving (May 4–26, 2026; n=25,873 US adults, 8,209 caregivers; released Nov 19) finds 29% of US adults provided unpaid care to a friend or family member in the past year — 32% of women vs 26% of men, and caregivers are 57% women, 42% men. Women caregivers are more likely to provide three or more types of assistance (40% vs 32%) and to spend 15+ hours a week (8% of all women vs 5% of men); 44% of caregivers are employed full-time. The toll: 66% report stress or worry, 35% worsening mental health, 25% financial strain — and it lands harder on women (mental health worsened for 38% of women caregivers vs 29% of men; physical health for 32% vs 26%). AARP's 2026 out-of-pocket study, cited in the same week's coverage, puts the average adult caregiver's spending at about $11,500 a year, 31% of household income. This is the mid-career squeeze's other half: the same 35–44 cohort the brain watches is the one carrying the care. | KFF · Nov 19, 2026 | [[caregiving-and-social-norms]] · 3.3 | ⬆️ | — |

| 5 | [The belonging gap has a curricular lever](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3793868): the ACM elective-design study (Communications of the ACM, Vol. 69, Issue 11, pp. 78–85, Oct 15, 2026) analyses 49,710 enrolments across 272 electives at nine institutions in Europe and North America over three academic years (2020–2023), using hierarchical Bayesian modelling. Women were more likely to enrol in electives on human-computer interaction, ethics and applied software development, and 59 of the 272 electives showed significant gender differences in enrolment. The authors' read: elective preferences can diagnose what the core curriculum hides, and aligning core and elective offerings with diverse interests — particularly socially relevant, human-centred computing — may improve equity. This answers the curator's Nov 26 question (file it as a catch-up?) with a yes: it is the belonging question's first curricular lever, sitting beside the Code.org outreach result and the UKESF exposure finding. | Communications of the ACM · Oct 15, 2026 | [[pipeline-and-education]] · 2.1 | ⬆️ | — |

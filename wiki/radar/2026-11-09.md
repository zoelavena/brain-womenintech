## Decisions for the curator

1. **A layoff-return gap, missed — Zety via Inc.com (Oct 1).** A survey of 1,000 workers laid off within the past two years found only 39% of women would work for their former employer again, vs 53% of men — a 14-point gap the piece ties to perceived unfairness and severance: 66% of women said they received no severance, vs 60% of men. The piece also notes women face higher layoff risk and that tightening return-to-office policies collide with care responsibilities. Published Oct 1, before the brain's window, so it surfaced only now. File it as a catch-up on workplace-policy / hiring-and-promotion-bias, or leave the recency window standing?
2. **The UK envoy's voice — FE News, Dr Anne-Marie Imafidon (Nov 2, updated Nov 9).** The Government's Women in Tech Envoy and Stemettes co-founder makes the pipeline case in her own words: women are 22% of the UK IT workforce, 2p of every £1 of UK VC goes to women-founded businesses, and the Government estimates enabling women to found and grow businesses at the same rate as men could unlock £310 billion. The piece also carries two figures new to the brain: the Government's call for evidence found 74% believe better workplace culture would support women's progression and leadership in tech, and a new scale-up women's programme is due to launch in 2027 (alongside the already-filed Tech Forward and Inclusive Investment Standards and the TechFirst Women's Programme). The article itself is dated Nov 2 — outside the window; the Nov 9 update is a site refresh, not new content. File it as a catch-up, and seat Imafidon at the Tea Party, or leave it?
3. **The NSF minority-STEM terminations get a dated, primary-source record — House letter (Oct 28).** Representatives Shontel Brown, Alma Adams and Luz Rivas wrote to the Department of Justice calling for reversal of the Aug 12, 2026 OLC opinion declaring three NSF STEM programmes unconstitutional and directing their termination: the Louis Stokes Alliances for Minority Participation (LSAMP), the Alliance for Graduate Education and the Professoriate (AGEP), and the Improving Undergraduate STEM Education programme (IUSE-HSI). The three programmes accounted for approximately $104 million of the $938 million Congress appropriated to NSF under the STEM Education account. The letter asks for answers by Nov 12, 2026. This is general STEM education, not tech, and dated Oct 28 — outside the window — but it is the datable, primary-source companion to the UltraViolet NSF grant-cut figures recorded (undatable, unfiled) on Nov 8. Track the terminations as workplace-policy context, or leave them out of scope?
4. **Still open from Nov 7–8, unanswered:** the ILO *Women in Business and Management: Scaling Success* catch-up, the Pew/Shorenstein/Danish AI catch-up (to which the Fortune "climbing wall" decision now attaches), the *Investing in Women Code Annual Report 2026* catch-up, the LinkedIn *Triple Penalty* catch-up, and the I Wish 2026 survey catch-up.

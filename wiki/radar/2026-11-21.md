| 1 | [The adoption gap has a third leg: risk perception](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03880): Fabian Stephany and Jedrzej Duszynski (University of Oxford) ask why men adopt GenAI more than women — and find the gap isn't only skills or access. Using two waves (2023–2024) of the nationally representative UK Public Attitudes to Data and AI Tracker (N ≈ 9,200), men report frequent personal GenAI use at 20.9% vs 15.5% for women — and the gap widens markedly among respondents concerned about AI's societal consequences, especially its effects on mental health and the environment. Perceived societal risk explains more of women's adoption behaviour than men's and ranks among the strongest predictors of women's GenAI use; the largest disparities (over 45 points) sit among younger, digitally fluent people with high societal-risk concerns. The hopeful edge: increases in optimism about AI's societal impact are associated with larger increases in women's uptake, narrowing the gap. A mechanism claim, not a rate — and a preprint (Research Square, May 21, 2026; arXiv v2, July 30, 2026), not yet peer-reviewed. | Oxford / arXiv · July 30, 2026 (v2) | [[ai-and-the-future-of-women-in-tech]] · 3.1, [[evidence-gap]] · 5.1 | ⬆️ | — |

| 4 | [Senior management slipped: women hold 32.9% of mid-market senior roles, down from 34.0%](https://www.grantthornton.global/en/insights/women-in-business/women-in-business-2026/): Grant Thornton's *Women in Business 2026: The value of visibility* (March 2026; International Business Report research, mid-market firms) puts women at 32.9% of senior management globally, down 1.1 points from 34.0% in 2025 — and on the trend line, parity in mid-market leadership is not projected until 2051. Businesses with all-male leadership teams rose from 4.1% to 5.7%. The long view still slopes up (+13.4 points over 22 years, from 19.4% in 2004), and the report notes increases in women holding various C-suite roles including CEO — but the year-on-year move is down, and the report is blunt that neither businesses nor the women in them can afford a further drop. | 5 | [The GenAI double bind: using the tool raises her competence and lowers her warmth](https://ifp.nyu.edu/2026/journal-article-abstracts/gwao-70275/): an experimental vignette study in *Information for Practice* (published Nov 13, 2026; 651 participants; 2 × 2 × 2 design — gender × GenAI adoption × task type, teaching vs research) finds GenAI adoption generally enhances perceived competence — and women gain the most in research tasks, where technical skill challenges gendered expectations. The authors call it a double bind: the same move that proves her competence costs her warmth, inside academic prestige hierarchies where both are judged. ## 🌱 Didn't fit the tree
- **The money gap follows her into retirement** (Invest Like Her, *The Money Gap*, Nov 2026 — a secondary roundup of StatsCan, HOOPP, CIRO and Ontario Pay Equity Office figures): Canadian women earned 88 cents for every dollar men earned in average hourly wages in 2025 (Statistics Canada); 68% of unretired women feel unprepared for retirement vs 53% of men (HOOPP Canadian Retirement Survey, April 2026); women receive 83 cents of retirement income for every dollar men receive, a gap unchanged since 1976 (Ontario Pay Equity Office, May 2024, 2021 data); only 47% of women feel confident about investing vs 66% of men (CIRO, *Women and Investing*, Nov 2025). The pay gap has a tail — it follows her from the paycheque into retirement and investing. But it's general women's finance, not women in tech, and every number here is a restatement of a source the brain hasn't filed. Curator decision: does the brain track the pay gap's tail into retirement, or keep the ruler on work?

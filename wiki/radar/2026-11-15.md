| 1 | [The confidence paradox (Singapore)](https://futurecio.tech/commentary-if-you-feel-super-confident-about-your-role-in-ai-youre-likely-male-and-this-needs-to-change/): young women use AI weekly (84%, 37% daily) and understand it — 75% show a core foundational understanding, and fewer hold misconceptions than men (25% vs 34%) — yet only 6% feel very confident they will thrive in an AI-driven environment vs 16% of men, only 8% see themselves as AI leaders or guides vs 20% of men, and only 21% believe AI careers are equally open to both genders vs 50% of men. On the work side, 38% of women use AI to support their work vs 30% of men. | 2 | [Ireland: the gender gap in GenAI use is small — and doesn't widen with intensity](https://www.economy-ni.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-11/9119%20WIS%20GenAI%20Report_0.pdf): across the island of Ireland, 29.0% of men and 27.8% of women intentionally use GenAI at work; adjusted for education, occupation, sector, firm size, contract type and age, women are about 4 points less likely to use it (−3.6, p ≈ 0.05). The gap does *not* widen as use deepens — frequency, multi-tool use and intensity differences run along occupational and educational lines, not gender — which contrasts sharply with the US evidence where men use multiple tools and with more intensity. Two more edges: employer training is thin (only 42.8% of users in the Republic and 37.7% in Northern Ireland have received or are receiving employer training), and the productivity gains "are flowing predominantly to employers and not to workers." Adoption is steeply stratified by earnings: 8.5% of workers earning under €15,000 use GenAI vs 73.8% of those earning €110,000+. Working in Ireland Survey 2025, nationally representative sample of 4,300 workers (3,040 Republic, 1,260 Northern Ireland). | 3 | [The support fix, measured (Denmark)](https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/inequality-in-ai-adoption-02257fc8d883): in the Danish working paper behind the PNAS adoption study, employer encouragement cut the gender gap in ever-use of ChatGPT from 11.9 to 5 percentage points, and employer-provided training cut it to 3.6 points. This is the first measured dose of the fix the brain has been waiting for since Nov 14 — encouragement more than halves the gap, training cuts it by two-thirds. Correction to the Nov 14 log: these cuts come from the Danish working paper (Humlum & Vestergaard, BFI Working Paper 2025–56), not from the Irish report. | 4 | [Equal Pay Day moved backwards (UK)](https://www.fawcettsociety.org.uk/News/press-release-equal-pay-day-moves-backwards): the mean full-time gender pay gap rose from 10.9% to 11.5%, so Equal Pay Day 2026 falls on Friday 20 November — two days earlier than last year — and women effectively work without pay for the final 42 days of the year. Mean full-time hourly pay excluding overtime: £26.90 (men) vs £23.81 (women), from the ONS Annual Survey of Hours and Earnings (provisional). Fawcett calls for salaries in every job advert, a ban on salary-history questions, and a legal Right to Know where unequal pay is suspected — and warns AI in recruitment, shift allocation, pay setting and promotion risks reproducing historic discrimination.

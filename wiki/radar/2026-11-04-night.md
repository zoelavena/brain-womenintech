## 1. Equal pay for equal work, nearly — and a 20% gap anyway

Ravio's 2027 Compensation Trends report, as summarized on its own page (first published Oct 2025, last updated Nov 4, 2026), puts the European tech gender pay gap at **20% unadjusted and 1% adjusted in 2026** — the unadjusted figure unchanged from 2025, the adjusted figure down from 2%. Ravio's read: with pay for the same role at the same level now close to equal, the headline 20% is driven by where women sit, not what they're paid once they're there.

- **Where it widens:** Engineering has the largest gap of any function — 21% unadjusted, 5% adjusted — and the highest adjusted gap of any function. Women make up just 19% of Engineering. By level, the unadjusted gap climbs through individual-contributor levels and peaks at M2 (Manager) at 15%, then narrows at senior management and executive level (3% at executive).
- **The executive package gap:** women hold 22% of executive roles, and those who do are paid less across every part of the package — 3–8% less in base salary, 21–28% less in bonus, 18–27% less in annual equity value, with the gap widening at C-level (median C-level base £180,000 vs £194,900; annual equity £91,600 vs £125,000).
- **The action gap:** 44% of companies took no specific action on their gender pay gap in the last 12 months. The most common action was adjusting individual salaries (34%); only 8% changed promotion or hiring practices — even though the surveyed People and Reward leaders named underrepresentation in senior roles (36%) and in higher-paying functions (26%) as the top drivers. On EU Pay Transparency Directive readiness, only 30% have calculated and documented their gap by worker category and 17% have a remediation plan; 52% say the biggest barrier is waiting for local law to be finalised.
- **Scope and sample:** European tech. The article body says salary data from 1,600+ European tech companies; the page's source line cites 1,800+ companies and 618,000+ employee datapoints plus a survey of 150 People and Reward Leaders. Both figures are on the same page and don't reconcile — filed here with the discrepancy noted, not smoothed over.

## 2. Caregiving, priced over a lifetime

AARP's Public Policy Institute published *The Hidden Cost of Caregiving: Lost Earnings, Retirement Income, and Social Security Income* on Nov 4, 2026 (Caldera, Forden & Palmieri), using the Urban Institute's DYNASIM4 model for US caregivers born 1981–1985. The average working caregiver loses **$120,500 in lifetime income** — $105,100 in earnings, $7,300 in employment-based retirement income, $8,100 in Social Security — and nationwide the losses total an estimated **$2.3 trillion**. Almost all working caregivers lose work hours; only 3.4% lost none.

**Scope label, stated plainly:** this is a general-workforce report, not a women-in-tech source. The AARP summary page as read gives no gender breakdown, so nothing from it is filed as a receipt on the caregiving theme. It is filed as a context evidence row: it prices the mechanism the brain's existing receipts already name — caring breaks that return women at lower levels and pay (Deloitte: 30% back at the same level and pay after 3+ years) and the parenthood penalty (Women in Digital: 41% mid-career). Whether the lifetime cost falls harder on women is the question the full report PDF may answer — see the curator decision below.

## 🧠 The brain's read

Ravio sharpens the ruler correction from Nov 3 rather than blurring it: a 1% adjusted gap next to a 20% unadjusted gap is the cleanest version yet of "equal work is nearly equally paid; equal access to the work is not." And it gives the incentives question its bluntest data point so far: companies can now *name* representation as the cause (36% say so) and still do nothing about it (44% took no action; 8% touched promotion or hiring). Transparency is arriving — the EU directive is forcing the measurement — but the measurement is not yet forcing the fix. AARP adds the other side of the same coin: the cost of the care load lands in lifetime earnings and retirement, not just this year's promotion round.

Third run today, a single finding at Zoe's direction: "AI judged male-associated prompts more competent." Verified at the paper itself (arXiv) and the Johns Hopkins release, not just the coverage.

| # | Finding | Outlet · date | Theme / node | Direction | Women of voice |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [AI judges male-associated prompts more competent](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13328v1): Johns Hopkins researchers rewrote real workplace prompts (427: emails, job applications, resignation letters) with women- vs men-associated linguistic features and ran them through GPT-4, Llama, Gemma, and Mistral. Women-associated prompts (hedges, tag questions, collective reference, expressive adjectives) drew shorter, less sophisticated, less formal responses across all four models, even after controlling for prompt complexity and style carry-over. Signing with a male name had virtually no effect. In a preliminary LLM-as-judge check (GPT-4 judge, 105 comparisons), the male-associated output was picked as more competent in 69.4% of decided comparisons and smarter in 68.3% — but the judge tied most of the time (67 ties on competence), and the authors call the analysis partial and single-sample. | Van Koevering & Field, Johns Hopkins, COLM 2026 (paper, Aug 2026); JHU Hub · Sept 21, 2026; Korn Ferry account · Nov 2, 2026 | [[ai-and-the-future-of-women-in-tech]] · 3.1, [[hiring-and-promotion-bias]] · 3.5 | ⬆️ | Katherine Van Koevering, Anjalie Field |

## Caveats carried into the wiki
- The 69.4%/68.3% figures are shares of *decided* comparisons only (36 decided on competence; 67 ties). The judge analysis is partial, GPT-4-only, single-sample — the authors label it preliminary.
- Prompts were rewritten by GPT-4 to inject the features; human validation judged 95.0% of sampled pairs to preserve the same task. Rewritten prompts rated slightly less realistic than real ones (3.84 vs 4.37 on a 5-point scale).
- The study uses a binary framing the authors flag themselves: the features are not gender-exclusive, and results hold for anyone who uses these patterns.

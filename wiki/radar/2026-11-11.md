## Decisions for the curator

1. **ISACA's authority gap — the verification gap from Nov 4 is now closed at the primary source. File it as a catch-up?** ISACA's own infographic for *Tech Workplace and Culture 2026* (and its ISACA Now blog, "The Authority Gap") confirms the figures the brain left unfiled on Nov 4 for being secondary-only: women are less likely than men to say they have sufficient authority over contribution to company strategy (53% vs 66%), personnel decisions (48% vs 59%), and purchasing decisions (32% vs 52%) — the purchasing split is a 20-point gap. The same study puts progression on par: 73% of women vs 71% of men received a salary increase or promotion in the past two years. Survey of 1,025 tech professionals globally, fielded July 17–Aug 3, 2026; findings published Oct 13, 2026. The pairing is the story — promoted at the same rate, with less say over strategy, hiring, and spending — and it lands squarely on the exposure gap (voice without resources, node 3.4) and the gatekeeping thesis. File the authority figures as a catch-up onto [[hiring-and-promotion-bias]] and [[exposure-gap]], or leave the recency window standing?
2. **ICTC's House of Commons testimony — file as a catch-up?** On Nov 2, 2026, ICTC's Lissa Matyas appeared before the House of Commons Standing Committee on the Status of Women for its study on the impact of AI on women, girls, and 2SLGBTQI+ individuals. The transcript (read in full at the primary source) carries figures new to the brain, from ICTC research across 505 occupations: women hold 62.7% of employment in the 10% of occupations with the highest AI task exposure; 64.3% of employed women aged 15–24 work in occupations where AI is far more likely to automate and substitute their core tasks entirely rather than augment them; women were 32.9% of Canada's digital economy workforce in Q2 2026 and 30% in leadership roles — "not increased over the last 20 years"; and among 55 digital occupations, women held just 10.6% of jobs combining high AI exposure with greater potential for AI to complement human work. AI appeared in 26.5% of digital-economy job postings by June 2026, more than four times a year earlier. This is the entry-level-door question with a Canadian receipt, and the 10.6% figure is the complement side of the exposure gap. Dated Nov 2 — outside tonight's window, missed by the Nov 4–10 runs. File it as a catch-up onto [[ai-and-the-future-of-women-in-tech]] and [[pipeline-and-education]], or leave it?

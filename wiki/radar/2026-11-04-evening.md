## 1. Three work trends, one narrowing door

The World Economic Forum's *3 Work Trends* newsletter, Issue 143 (published Nov 4, 2026), covers three stories: the value of human expertise, preparing for careers in an AI world, and helping young people find lasting employment.

- **Should experts training AI share in what it earns?** Lawyers, mathematicians, and other specialists are paid to help AI models improve their reasoning — but the knowledge they share could help automate parts of their own work. The issue asks whether payment should end when the assignment does, or whether experts should retain a stake in the value their knowledge creates. Marta Golin (PhD) explores proposals for "knowledge guilds" that would let workers negotiate collectively over how their expertise is used and how returns are shared. These remain proposals, with open questions about tracking contributions and dividing returns fairly. The wider challenge it names: as AI takes on more entry-level tasks, how will the next generation gain the experience needed to become experts themselves?
- **How can young people prepare for work in a world of AI?** As AI changes entry-level work, young people face uncertainty about getting started and standing out. Arun Sundararajan, Professor of Entrepreneurship at NYU Stern, offers advice on a Radio Davos episode: build human networks and become an AI "force multiplier," with three recommendations in the episode.
- **Why does tackling youth unemployment take more than training?** An automotive supplier in Tunisia needed 700 operators and was ready to train them itself — its immediate problem was finding enough people. Youth employment programmes often fund training with less support for reaching recruits and connecting them with employers. Drawing on Tunisia's FBR@WORK programme, Anis Saidi of the Education Outcomes Fund sets three priorities for governments and employers: reach potential recruits, including young people not connected to employment services; connect preparation to available jobs, with chances to learn technical skills at work; and support new employees through their first weeks.

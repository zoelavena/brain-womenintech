## Decisions for the curator

1. **The Washington Post amplifies the Pew AI-exposure finding — file the Pew receipt now?** The Post's Nov 7 story, "Men see more career advancement in jobs with AI, as women lose ground," reports Pew Research Center's Oct 22 analysis *Men Are Experiencing More Job Growth Than Women in AI-Exposed Occupations*: for each 1-point increase in an occupation's AI-exposure score, men's employment grew 6.3% while women's barely changed (0.2%), comparing 2017–19 with January 2023–June 2026 (CPS data, O*NET exposure scores; 2020–22 excluded). Men in the most AI-exposed occupations saw about 24% more employment growth than in the least exposed; women's growth was about the same across the scale. This is the same figure already in the Nov 8 catch-up decision (Fortune's "climbing wall" piece); the Post story is a second major amplifier and reframes it as advancement, not just employment growth. The Post story itself is paywalled — only secondary summaries (HTT News, Nov 9) were read — but the underlying Pew figure is now confirmed at the Pew page itself (see "Could not verify"). The Pew analysis is dated Oct 22, so it stays outside tonight's window either way. File the Pew receipt with the Post as the amplifier, or leave the recency window standing?
2. **WEF's "drop to the top" — parenting-break promotion figures (Nov 3, secondary).** A Slayton Search piece (Nov 3) drawing on the WEF Global Gender Gap Report 2026 and LinkedIn Economic Graph data carries figures new to the brain: among senior individual contributors returning from a full-time parenting break, men are 44% more likely than women to be promoted in their first year back; three years after returning, 7.2% of women had moved up in seniority vs 10.0% of men; women are more than four times as likely as men to take a full-time parenting break (26.5% vs 6.0%); and women's share of new hires into top-level management has fallen every year since 2022, from 34.8% (the piece cuts off before the current figure). The WEF framing is that women's representation halves on the way to the C-suite (46% of entry-level workers, 23% of C-suite). Dated Nov 3 and secondary — the WEF report itself was not opened. File the parenting-break figures in a catch-up with primary verification at WEF, or leave them?
3. **Women in Tech Sweden, *State of Women in Tech 2026* (June 2026) — catch-up?** The association's annual member survey (2,807 respondents, 98.8% women, 8 in 10 currently working in tech): 88% give the two highest ratings for enjoying working in tech — 51.8% the highest rating, down from 53.8% in 2025. The report also cites the McKinsey March 2026 finding that women's share of core tech roles in Europe fell three points during 2025, and notes Sweden has not implemented the EU Pay Transparency Directive on its original timeline. A national member survey, dated June — outside the window. File it in a catch-up, or leave it?

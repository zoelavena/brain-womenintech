## Decisions for the curator

1. **ILO *Women in Business and Management: Scaling Success* — catch-up?** Released 3 November 2026 and missed by the Nov 4–6 runs. It is the strongest incentives evidence the brain has seen in a while: in the ILO's 2025 survey of 11,957 enterprises across 99 countries, 68.5% reported better business outcomes from gender equality and diversity initiatives (up 11.1 points since 2018); enterprises led by a woman CEO are 6.4% more likely to report better business outcomes than those led by a man, and 17.5% more likely with a woman board chair. But implementation fell — the share of enterprises running such initiatives dropped from 37.4% in 2018 to 26.5% in 2025, while 74.8% hold a policy. And the pipeline underneath the top thinned: women are 28.2% of CEOs (up from 23.4% in 2013) and 30.9% of board chairs (up from 13.1%), yet still 30% of management overall, with gender balance improving at senior and top levels and declining at junior and middle management. Figures verified at the ILO's executive summary. General-workforce scope (all sectors, 99 countries), not tech — file it in a curator-directed catch-up with a scope label, or leave the recency window standing?
2. **The AI-gender catch-up grows.** Pew Research Center's *The Gender Gap in AI* (Oct 16, 2026; verified at Pew, not just the Nov 7 LinkedIn commentary that surfaced it) joins the still-open Harvard Shorenstein question (Oct 14): the access gap has closed — 50% of men and 47% of women have used AI chatbots (Feb 2026 survey, up from 39% vs 28% in 2024) — but the confidence and trust gaps haven't: men use chatbots daily 27% vs 20%, say chatbots help their productivity 35% vs 25%, and women are about twice as likely to expect AI to affect them personally negatively rather than positively over 20 years (33% vs 17%), with 68% vs 58% saying AI is advancing too quickly. And a Nov 6 Medium synthesis of the Danish PNAS study (Humlum & Vestergaard) reports the mechanism that answers the brain's open question about what counts as proof: employer encouragement cut the ChatGPT use gap from 11.9 to 5 percentage points, and employer-provided training cut it to 3.6. File Pew, Shorenstein, and the Danish study in one AI catch-up, or leave them?
3. **Investing in Women Code Annual Report 2026 (Oct 7) — catch-up?** The UK voluntary code's signatories directed 32% of their venture capital to teams with at least one female founder — more than double the wider market — and 6% to all-female founding teams vs 2% in the wider market, while women-led businesses now achieve equal loan approval rates but apply less often (37% vs 49%) and request smaller amounts. A voluntary code outperforming the market is the incentives question with a live test case. File it in a catch-up, or leave it?

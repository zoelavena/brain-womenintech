# 🛰️ Radar: 2026-11-23

Scheduled nightly run. Window: everything published since the Nov 22 run, plus catch-up. The headline is the glass cliff closing in eleven days: Nvidia's board brought Jensen Huang back six days after ruling out his return, and the women NPR could reach mostly wouldn't talk on the record. Underneath it, three mechanisms got sharper: the AI risk-perception leg is now peer-reviewed and experimental, the developer pay gap splits by income level — glass ceiling in rich countries, sticky floor in poorer ones — and belonging now has a second witness as the choice gap's mechanism.

| 2 | [The risk-perception leg is now peer-reviewed — and experimental](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12817214/): Borwein, Magistro, Alvarez, Bonikowski and Loewen (PNAS Nexus, Jan 20, 2026) surveyed ~3,049 Americans and Canadians (YouGov, Nov 2023) and find women consistently perceive AI as riskier than men — 11% more likely to say its risks outweigh its benefits (4.87 vs 4.38 on a 0–10 scale). Two drivers: higher general risk aversion (lottery questions) and greater exposure to AI-related employment risk. The experiment makes it causal, not correlational: as the stated probability of net positive employment effects falls, women's support for a company adopting AI falls more sharply than men's — but when net benefits are guaranteed, women support adoption nearly as strongly as men. Women were also 6–7 points more likely to say they "don't know" AI's benefits. The paper notes men's research productivity rose 6.4% more than women's after ChatGPT's release. This is the Stephany & Duszynski mechanism (filed Nov 21 as a preprint) with peer review and a causal test behind it. | PNAS Nexus · Jan 20, 2026 | [[ai-and-the-future-of-women-in-tech]] · 3.1, [[evidence-gap]] | ⬆️ | [[beatrice-magistro\|Beatrice Magistro]] (seated, brewing) |
| 3 | [One pay gap, two shapes: glass ceiling in rich countries, sticky floor in poorer ones](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12651-025-00407-z): Prakash & Yadav (Journal for Labour Market Research, 2025, Article 17) analyse 53,507 developers across 173 countries (2022 Stack Overflow Developer Survey) and find women developers earn 17.6% less than men after adjusting for human capital, technical skills and firm characteristics. The gap splits by income level: 8.8% in high-income economies, 22.9% upper-middle, 36.6% lower-middle — and 47.9% in South Asia. In high-income countries the gap widens toward the top (a glass ceiling: the top-decile coefficient reaches −0.1968 unconditional); in lower-middle-income countries it is worst at the bottom (a sticky floor: −0.4444 at the first decile). The unexplained share runs 45% in high-income countries and 68.5% elsewhere (pooled Oaxaca-Blinder). Women stay concentrated in QA, testing and client-facing roles rather than systems engineering or executive leadership. A payroll pay-gap number, not a perception one — but on 2022 survey data, self-reported, and binary-gendered. | Journal for Labour Market Research · 2025 (summary Nov 20, 2026) | [[hiring-and-promotion-bias]] · 3.5, [[organizational-measurement]] | ⬆️ | — |

| 6 | [Belonging gets a second witness — and it starts at twelve](https://csforall.org/en-US/csiseverything/white-paper/perceptions-of-computer-science-education): Code.org and CSforALL's perceptions survey (YouGov, Sep–Nov 2024; 1,019 US students aged 12–17) finds feeling personally connected to computer science is by far the biggest driver of students calling it important — accounting for 60% of the importance attributed to CS — and for middle-school girls the top drivers are that CS relates to things they're interested in and that it is "for someone like me."

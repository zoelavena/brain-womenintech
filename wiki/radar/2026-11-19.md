| 4 | [Cut flexibility and 42% of women start looking for a new job](https://www.onrec.com/news/employee-experience-wellbeing/reduced-flexibility-could-push-two-in-five-women-to-leave-robert): Robert Walters' Employee Benefits Guide (Oct 22, 2026; 3,100 white-collar professionals across the UK and Ireland) finds 42% of female professionals would start looking for a new job if their flexibility was reduced, vs 34% of men — and 72% of women rank flexibility as highly important, vs 49% of men. | 5 | [The pay-quartile ladder: women are 48% of the lowest-paid quarter, 25% of the highest](https://ravio.com/blog/women-in-tech): further cuts from Ravio's Compensation Trends 2027 (the report itself already filed Nov 4 and Nov 6; blog "Women in tech statistics 2026", updated Nov 4, 2026) split the European tech workforce into four equal pay bands: women are 48% of the lowest-paid quarter, 40% of the second, 30% of the third, and 25% of the highest-paid quarter. The country splits: UK 51% → 26%, Germany 45% → 22%, Netherlands 50% → 25%, France 49% → 26%, Spain 51% → 28%, Sweden 48% → 28% — Sweden is the one market that breaks the pattern, its top quartile (28% women) more balanced than its third (23%).
